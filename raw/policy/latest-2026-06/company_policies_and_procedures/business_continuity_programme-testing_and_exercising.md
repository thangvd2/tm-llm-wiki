# Business Continuity Management Programme: Testing and Exercising Summary 2025: Third Party Information Notice

[Download PDF](/policy/latest/EN/resources/business_continuity_programme-testing_and_exercising.pdf)

## [](#introduction "Copy link to heading")1\. Introduction

### [](#context "Copy link to heading")1.1. Context

Thought Machine ™ is committed to ensuring robust business continuity measures are in place across the business in order to maintain predefined levels of acceptable product and service delivery, as well as relevant underlying internal supporting processes and functions, in the event of disruption arising from an incident or crisis.

### [](#document_purpose "Copy link to heading")1.2. Document Purpose

The purpose of this document is to provide internal and external interested parties with a high level summary of the last calendar year’s business continuity testing and exercising activities and results, along with identified opportunities for improvement in plans and processes.

## [](#testing_and_exercising_approach "Copy link to heading")2\. Testing and Exercising Approach

The business continuity testing and exercising programme aims to validate that the organisation has the capability to respond and operate effectively under business continuity conditions, avoiding maximum acceptable outage (MAO) deadlines, achieving recovery time (RTO) and recovery point (RPO) objectives where expected, and performing within any SLAs in place. In turn, testing and exercising supports continual improvement of plans, processes and the wider BCMS through identifying areas for improvement (e.g. procedures, training needs, resource requirements etc).

Testing and exercising takes various forms including discussion exercises, tabletops, simulations and actual live incidents. This is conducted at planned intervals throughout the year in response to product/service delivery priorities, or after a significant change in the organisation or the context it is operating within.

Exercises are linked to risks identified in the BC Risk Register and the results of every exercise or test conducted are reported to the BC Steering Committee for review and agreement of any remediation actions that may be required.

The cadence of training and exercising allows for gradual improvement over time. Relevant business continuity plans are exercised annually, and the business continuity plan in its entirety is verified, reviewed and evaluated across a 2 to 3 year period.
