## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")1\. Purpose

Thought Machine Group Limited and its global subsidiaries (collectively “**Thought Machine**”, “**we**”, “**us**”, “**our**”) is committed to meeting the highest standards for the protection of Personal Data, protecting the privacy of our Personnel, Clients, End-Customers, suppliers and partners. This Data Protection Policy (the “**Policy**”) establishes the global standards that are designed to meet and exceed our global legal, regulatory and contractual obligations for the protection of Personal Data. Thought Machine upholds its obligations to protect entrusted Personal Data through rigorous Processing standards and compliance with all relevant data protection laws.

The purpose of the policy is to:

1.  establish the minimum standards for Processing Personal Data, which are designed to ensure compliance with data protection laws and regulations across all relevant jurisdictions;
    
2.  assist Thought Machine and its Personnel in fulfilling our data protection obligations;
    
3.  safeguard the rights of individuals whose Personal Data we Process (including Personnel, Clients, End-Customers, suppliers and partners); and
    
4.  mitigate risks associated with Personal Data Breaches, protecting both the organisation and individuals from financial, reputational, legal or other harm.
    

Throughout this Policy, certain terms are capitalised (e.g., “*Personal Data*” and “*Processing*”). Please refer to 4. Definitions for their specific meanings.

## [](#definitions "Copy link to heading")4\. Definitions

-   **AI**: A machine-based system designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy that infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments.
    
-   **Anonymisation**: To irreversibly prevent identification of individuals within a data set by removing all parts of the Personal Data that allow identification of a person. If done correctly, you will never be able to re-identify the person. Truly anonymised data is out of scope of data protection laws.
    
-   **Client**: Any third party (e.g., bank, financial institution) and/or partner that has signed a contract to access and use Thought Machine’s products or services (not a Data Subject/End-Customer).
    
-   **Client Data**: All data Processed by Thought Machine in relation to products or services provided to Clients (not just Personal Data).
    
-   **Data Controller**: The entity (either an individual or organisation) which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the Processing of Personal Data, i.e., why it is processed and how.
    
-   **Data Processor**: The entity (either an individual or organisation) responsible for Processing Personal Data on behalf of a Data Controller.
    
-   **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)**: A process to help an organisation identify and minimise privacy and data protection risks of a business initiative. In certain circumstances, it is a legal requirement to conduct a DPIA prior to undertaking Processing activities, i.e., where Processing may result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of Data Subjects.
    
-   **Data Protection Officer**: A person appointed by an organisation to perform this role, as defined by Article 39 of the EU/UK GDPR.
    
-   **Data Subject**: Any living individual who can be identified from the Personal Data being Processed. This includes Personnel, Clients (and prospective clients), supplier and partner representatives, and End-Customers.
    
-   **End-Customer**: Our Clients’ end-customers to whom the Client provides regulated financial services, directly or indirectly, through the use of Thought Machine’s products or services and whose Personal Data is processed by such products or services.
    
-   **Enterprise-Level Personal Data**: Personal Data related to the operation of Thought Machine’s business, including in relation to Personnel (such as human resources and payroll data) and Client, supplier and partner representative Personal Data.
    
-   **Personal Data**: Any information that relates to an individual (or Data Subject) who can be identified directly or indirectly (i.e., when it is combined with other information) by the information in question. Examples of Personal Data include, but are not limited to: name, job title, age, residential and email address, IP address (and other online identifiers), financial data (including payment details, bank account details and cardholder data) and vehicle registration number. This also includes Pseudonymised data (where identifiers are replaced by codes), as this is still legally considered Personal Data. The term “PII” is often used instead of Personal Data, but Personal Data is a legally defined and much broader category of information that data protection laws say we must protect.
    
-   **Personal Data Incident**: An incident involving Personal Data that has either not yet been confirmed as a Personal Data Breach or does not meet the criteria to be a Personal Data Breach.
    
-   **Personal Data Breach**: A confirmed breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, Personal Data. This includes breaches that are the result of both accidental and deliberate causes.
    
-   **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)**: A subset of Personal Data that specifically focuses on information used to uniquely identify, contact or locate an individual, such as an identification number (e.g., passport or social security number (SSN)) or residential address. All PII is Personal Data but not all Personal Data is PII.
    
-   **Personnel**: Any employee or officer of Thought Machine, and any individual personnel contracted for services to Thought Machine, whether directly or through a third party, whether on a fixed term, indefinite or temporary basis.
    
-   **Processing**: All actions or handling of Personal Data by manual or automated means, e.g., obtaining, recording, holding, or carrying out any operation on personal data. It includes; organisation or alteration; retrieval or use; disclosure and anonymisation, blocking or destruction. Most use of Personal Data will constitute “Processing”.
    
-   **Production-Level Personal Data**: Personal Data (often PII) that Thought Machine Processes on behalf of its Clients (including Personal Data/PII relating to End-Customers).
    
-   **Pseudonymisation**: The process of replacing identifying information (like a name or ID number) with a "pseudonym" or a false identifier. It is often confused with Anonymisation but with Pseudonymisation the individual can still ultimately be identified, for example if you connect this information with other information held elsewhere. Data protection laws apply to Pseudonymised Personal Data - it must be protected in the same manner as non-pseudonymised Personal Data.
    
-   **Record of Processing Activity (RoPA)**: An internal record of Personal Data Processing activities, which serves as a comprehensive map of how an organisation handles Personal Data.
    
-   **Special Category Personal Data**: Personal Data that is more sensitive in nature and therefore requires more protection. This includes data relating to: health, race, ethnicity, religious or philosophical beliefs, political opinions, trade union membership, genetic or biometric data (when used to identify you) and information about your sex life and sexual orientation.
    
-   **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)**: Clauses for data transfers from the EU and UK to non-EU countries.
    
-   **Sub-Processor**: An entity (either an individual or organisation) that a Data Processor hires to help Process Personal Data, for example a cloud service provider. The original Data Processor remains fully liable to the Data Controller for the Sub-Processor’s compliance, requiring a contract with equivalent data protection terms, authorisation from the Data Controller, and sufficient technical and organisational measures to ensure security and compliance.
    
-   **Technical and Organisational Measures (TOMs)**: Data protection laws state that organisations must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to keep Personal Data secure.
    

## [](#controller_and_processor "Copy link to heading")5\. Controller and Processor

Thought Machine acts as a Data Controller when Processing Enterprise-Level Personal Data and a Data Processor when Processing Production-Level Personal Data, and shall have policies, standards, processes and procedures in place to comply with the requirements of all applicable data protection laws and regulations.

## [](#data_protection_principles "Copy link to heading")6\. Data Protection Principles

1.  Thought Machine will Process Personal Data lawfully, including identifying an appropriate lawful basis prior to Processing Personal Data.
    
2.  Where Thought Machine Processes Special Category Personal Data, it will further ensure that a specific condition for Processing is identified and documented, and enhanced security measures are applied.
    
3.  Thought Machine will Process Personal Data fairly and in a transparent manner, including by maintaining and publishing up-to-date privacy notices describing its Processing activities in accordance with the transparency principle.
    
4.  Thought Machine will collect Personal Data for specified and limited purposes, and the collected Personal Data will only be used for those specified purposes.
    
5.  Thought Machine will only collect Personal Data that is necessary to fulfil the purposes for which it is being collected.
    
6.  Thought Machine will endeavour to keep the Personal Data it Processes accurate, relevant, sufficiently complete and up-to-date to support its position as a service provider or employer.
    
7.  Thought Machine will retain and process Personal Data for no longer than is necessary to fulfil the purposes for which it is collected, subject to any overriding legal obligations that apply in respect of such Personal Data, and, where applicable, in accordance with the Records Management Policy and the Records Retention and Disposition Schedule.
    
8.  Thought Machine will have in place appropriate technical, organisational and physical measures to keep Personal Data secure, and in accordance with the Information Security Policy. Thought Machine will regularly review and update its information security policies, standards, processes and procedures to ensure they continue to meet or exceed established good practice at all times.
    

## [](#accountability "Copy link to heading")7\. Accountability

1.  Thought Machine will record and maintain the purposes for which the Personal Data is collected in the Record of Processing Activities (RoPA).
    
2.  Thought Machine will consider and, where applicable, apply privacy and data protection methodologies in its processes, systems, products and services that undertake Processing activities, utilising the principles of data protection by design and default and established good practice.
    
3.  In alignment with regulatory guidance, Thought Machine will review the need for, and where appropriate conduct, a Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA) whenever a new Processing purpose is identified and legitimate interests is the lawful basis relied upon, to ensure a balanced and lawful basis for data usage.
    
4.  Thought Machine will conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) in accordance with its internal process, whenever Processing activities pose a high risk to individuals, are legally mandated, or align with regulatory guidance and best practice.
    
5.  Thought Machine will undertake appropriate due diligence prior to onboarding any third-party Data Processors in accordance with the Third Party Risk Management Policy. Thought Machine will, as necessary, obtain ongoing assurances, during the term of the relevant contract, that onboarded third-party Data Processors are fulfilling their contractual obligations and handling Thought Machine’s Personal Data appropriately.
    
6.  Thought Machine shall establish and maintain a written contract with the relevant third-party Data Controllers when sharing Personal Data with such parties.
    
7.  Thought Machine, via the DPO, shall maintain its registration(s) with relevant supervisory authorities and ensure any annual fees are paid.
    

## [](#individual_rights "Copy link to heading")8\. Individual Rights

1.  When acting as a Data Controller, Thought Machine maintains processes to respond to requests for the exercise of individual rights by Data Subjects. These include: the right to be informed, access, rectification and erasure, in accordance with our internal individual rights procedures.
    
2.  When acting as a Data Processor, Thought Machine has processes in place to enable Data Controllers to lawfully respond to the legitimate exercise of individual rights by Data Subjects, the End-Customers. Thought Machine will do so in accordance with relevant internal individual rights procedures. The Client, as Data Controller, retains primary responsibility for responding to individual rights requests that they receive from End-Customers.
    

## [](#production_level_personal_data_thought_machine_as_a_data_processor "Copy link to heading")9\. Production-Level Personal Data (Thought Machine as a Data Processor)

At a production-level, Thought Machine processes Personal Data on behalf of our Clients as a Data Processor. In this case, the Data Subjects are the End-Customers of Thought Machine’s Clients.

### [](#transparency "Copy link to heading")9.1. Transparency

1.  Thought Machine will only Process Production-Level Personal Data provided by the Client, the Data Controller, in accordance with their documented instructions and agreed activities to maintain or provide the products and/or services.
    
2.  Thought Machine shall notify the Client, in accordance with its legal obligations, if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes applicable data protection law.
    
3.  Thought Machine does not perform automated decision-making based upon Production-Level Personal Data.
    

### [](#sub_processors "Copy link to heading")9.2. Sub-Processors

1.  Thought Machine uses a limited set of Sub-Processors. Where a Sub-Processor is used:
    
    1.  Authorisation for the use of any such Sub-Processor by Thought Machine, or changes to such Sub-Processor, shall be obtained from the Data Controller.
        
    2.  Thought Machine shall have a written contract in place when engaging Sub-processors to Process Personal Data on its behalf, and each such contract will include appropriate data protection obligations relating to Personal Data Processing as required by applicable data protection law.
        
    3.  Thought Machine will take reasonable steps to verify that the Sub-processor has appropriate technical and organisational measures, i.e., security, in place.
        
    

### [](#security_confidentiality_integrity_and_availability "Copy link to heading")9.3. Security (Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability)

1.  Thought Machine will not access, use or disclose to any third party Personal Data, except, in each case, as necessary to maintain or provide the products and/or services.
    
2.  As the Data Controller, the Client retains primary responsibility for the integrity and accuracy of Personal Data. Thought Machine supports the Client in meeting these obligations through its product architecture, in accordance with each product’s specifications.
    
3.  Thought Machine Personnel will only access Production-Level Personal Data on a strictly limited and need-to-know basis, as permissioned by the Data Controller, following, at all times, Thought Machine’s documented procedures for authorised access to a database.
    
4.  Thought Machine will not prevent a Client from accessing its Personal Data, subject to technological authorisations and in compliance with the product specifications and availability.
    
5.  Thought Machine will encrypt all Personal Data at rest and in transit to Sub-Processors, in line with internal procedures.
    

### [](#data_retention_destruction_and_storage_limitation "Copy link to heading")9.4. Data Retention, Destruction and Storage Limitation

1.  As the Data Controller, the Client retains primary responsibility for the retention of Personal Data. Thought Machine supports the Client in meeting these obligations.
    

## [](#personal_data_breaches "Copy link to heading")10\. Personal Data Breaches

1.  Thought Machine has established and will maintain processes to identify, report and manage Personal Data Incidents and Personal Data Breaches. Any such Incidents or Breaches involving Production-Level Personal Data, are managed in accordance with the Client PII Breach Reporting Procedure and the Security Incident Response Procedure.
    
2.  Thought Machine will review and ascertain whether identified Personal Data Incidents constitute Personal Data Breaches.
    
3.  In the event of a Personal Data Breach when acting as a Data Controller, Thought Machine will:
    
    1.  undertake a breach notification assessment to establish whether the applicable legal threshold to notify any relevant data protection supervisory authority has been met;
        
    2.  notify the supervisory authority of any Personal Data Breaches that meet the threshold within the applicable deadline required by law, and comply with its legal obligations in respect of such notification; and
        
    3.  where a Personal Data Breach necessitates notification under applicable data protection law, inform the affected Data Subjects in accordance with its legal obligations.
        
    
4.  In the event of a Personal Data Breach when Thought Machine acts as a Data Processor, we will fulfil all contractual obligations to our Clients (the Data Controllers), and act in accordance with applicable data protection laws.
    

## [](#international_transfers "Copy link to heading")11\. International Transfers

1.  When acting as a Data Controller:
    
    1.  Thought Machine will rely on suitable safeguards when transferring Personal Data to a country outside the UK, except where the country has been granted adequacy status (or similar, e.g., UK-US Data Privacy Framework (DPF) Extension certification). Where safeguards are required, Thought Machine will rely on International Data Transfer Agreements (IDTAs) or other approved Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs); and
        
    2.  If Thought Machine transfers Personal Data in reliance on IDTAs or SCCs, it will conduct risk assessments to ensure these mechanisms provide sufficient protection within the destination country’s legal framework. Where risks are identified, Thought Machine will implement supplementary measures to mitigate them.
        
    
2.  When acting as a Data Processor:
    
    1.  All Production-Level Personal Data will be maintained within the designated geographical region(s), in strict accordance with the terms of the Client contract.
        
    2.  Any transfer of Production-Level Personal Data to a third country is conducted solely as permitted by our Client contracts and applicable data protection laws.

### [](#australia_australian_privacy_principles_apps "Copy link to heading")17.1. Australia: Australian Privacy Principles (APPs)

The APPs define a number of key data protection obligations related to Personal Data:

1.  **Direct marketing**: If Thought Machine holds Personal Data about an individual, it must not use or disclose the information for the purpose of direct marketing unless the individual has consented to the use or disclosure of the information for that purpose.
    
2.  **Correction of Personal Data**: Thought Machine must take reasonable steps to correct information to ensure that, having regard to the purpose for which it is held, the information is accurate, up-to-date, complete, relevant and not misleading.
    
3.  **Quality of Personal Data**: Thought Machine must take reasonable steps to ensure that the Personal Data it collects is accurate, up-to-date and complete.
    
4.  **Means of collection**: Thought Machine must collect Personal Data only by lawful and fair means.
    
5.  **Notification of the collection of Personal Data**: Thought Machine must notify individuals of the purposes for which it is intending to collect, use or disclose their Personal Data on or before or as soon as practicable after, such collection, use or disclosure of Personal Data.
    
6.  **Cross border disclosure of Personal Data**: Before Thought Machine discloses Personal Data about an individual to a person (the overseas recipient) who is not in Australia or an external territory and who is not the entity or the individual, Thought Machine must take reasonable steps to ensure that the overseas recipient does not breach the APPs.
    
7.  **Security of Personal Data**: Thought Machine must take reasonable steps to protect the information:
    
    1.  from misuse, interference and loss; and
        
    2.  from unauthorised access, modification or disclosure.
        
    
8.  Thought Machine is prohibited from providing an individual access to Personal Data in the following circumstances:
    
    1.  it reasonably believes that giving access would pose a serious threat to the life, health or safety of any individual, or to public health or public safety;
        
    2.  giving access would have an unreasonable impact on the privacy of other individuals;
        
    3.  the request for access is frivolous or vexatious;
        
    4.  the information relates to existing or anticipated legal proceedings between the entity and the individual, and would not be accessible by the process of discovery in those proceedings;
        
    5.  giving access would reveal the intentions of Thought Machine in relation to negotiations with the individual in such a way as to prejudice those negotiations;
        
    6.  giving access would be unlawful;
        
    7.  denying access is required or authorised by or under an Australian law or a court/tribunal order;
        
    8.  both of the following apply:
        
        1.  Thought Machine has reason to suspect that unlawful activity, or misconduct of a serious nature, that relates to the entity’s functions or activities has been, is being or may be engaged in; and
            
        2.  giving access would be likely to prejudice the taking of appropriate action in relation to the matter.
            
        
    9.  giving access would be likely to prejudice one or more enforcement related activities conducted by, or on behalf of, an enforcement body; or
        
    10.  giving access would reveal evaluative information generated within Thought Machine in connection with a commercially sensitive decision-making process.
         
    

If an employee has provided consent for the Processing of Personal Data, they have the right (in certain circumstances) to withdraw that consent at any time which will not affect the lawfulness of the Processing before their consent was withdrawn. To revoke your consent, access Personal Data held by Thought Machine or to make a complaint about the Processing of your Personal Data, please contact Thought Machine’s DPO.

### [](#singapore_personal_data_protection_act_2012_pdpa "Copy link to heading")17.3. Singapore: Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (PDPA)

The PDPA defines nine key data protection obligations related to Personal Data:

1.  **Consent**: Only collect, use or disclose personal data for purposes for which an individual has given his or her consent. Allow individuals to withdraw consent, with reasonable notice, and inform them of the likely consequences of withdrawal. Upon withdrawal of consent to the collection, use or disclosure for any purpose, Thought Machine must cease such collection, use or disclosure of the personal data.
    
2.  **Purpose limitation**: Thought Machine may collect, use or disclose personal data about an individual for the purposes that a reasonable person would consider appropriate in the circumstances and for which the individual has given consent. Thought Machine may not, as a condition of providing a product or service, require the individual to consent to the collection, use or disclosure of his or her personal data beyond what is reasonable to provide that product or service.
    
3.  **Notification**: Thought Machine must notify individuals of the purposes for which it is intending to collect, use or disclose their personal data on or before such collection, use or disclosure of personal data.
    
4.  **Access and Correction**: Upon request, the personal data of an individual and information about the ways in which his or her personal data has been or may have been used or disclosed within a year before the request should be provided. However, Thought Machine is prohibited from providing an individual access if the provision of the personal data or other information could reasonably be expected to:
    
    1.  cause immediate or grave harm to the individual’s safety or physical or mental health;
        
    2.  threaten the safety or physical or mental health of another individual;
        
    3.  reveal personal data about another individual;
        
    4.  reveal the identity of another individual who has provided the personal data, and the individual has not consented to the disclosure of his or her identity; or
        
    5.  be contrary to Singapore’s national interest. Thought Machine is also required to correct any error or omission in an individual’s personal data upon his or her request. Unless Thought Machine is satisfied on reasonable grounds that the correction should not be made, it should correct the personal data as soon as practicable and send the corrected data to other organisations to which the personal data was disclosed within a year before the correction is made (or, with the individual’s consent, only to selected organisations).
        
    
5.  **Accuracy**: Make reasonable effort to ensure that personal data collected by or on behalf of Thought Machine is accurate and complete, if it is likely to be used to make a decision that affects the individual, or if it is likely to be disclosed to another organisation.
    
6.  **Protection**: Make reasonable security arrangements to protect the personal data that Thought Machine possesses or controls to prevent unauthorised access, collection, use, disclosure or similar risks.
    
7.  **Retention Limitation**: Cease retention of personal data or remove the means by which the personal data can be associated with particular individuals when it is no longer necessary for any business or legal purpose.
    
8.  **Transfer Limitation**: Transfer personal data to another country only according to the requirements prescribed under PDPA regulations, to ensure that the standard of protection provided to the personal data so transferred will be comparable to the protection under the PDPA.
    
9.  **Accountability**: Make information about Thought Machine’s data protection policies, practices and complaints process available on request. Designate one or more individuals as a Data Protection Officer to ensure that Thought Machine complies with the PDPA, including the implementation of personal data protection policies. The business contact information of the DPO should also be made available to the public.

### [](#us_state_privacy_legislation "Copy link to heading")17.4. US State Privacy Legislation

US state privacy legislation refers to current and upcoming comprehensive state data protection legislation, for example in California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah, Oregon, Texas and Montana. US state privacy legislation applies to Thought Machine only to the extent that the applicable state thresholds are met. Where the thresholds are met, Thought Machine will comply with the following general obligations:

1.  **Transparency**: All states generally require privacy notices to be made available to individuals containing relevant transparency information. For example, California has a notice-at-collection requirement, whereby organisations must give notice about the categories of personal data collected and the business purpose for doing so, along with a link to its “Do Not Sell” page and privacy notice. Virginia, Colorado and Connecticut require consent to process sensitive personal data.
    
2.  **Data security**: Thought Machine must maintain reasonable data security measures that vary in complexity depending on the state requirement. California requires security measures appropriate to the nature of the information requiring protection. Virginia and Connecticut require reasonable administrative, technical and physical data security practices to be appropriate to the volume and nature of the personal data at issue. Utah must not only protect the confidentiality and integrity of personal data but also reasonably reduce any foreseeable risks of harm to consumers, in an appropriate way for the controller’s business size, scope, and type, and the volume and nature of the personal data. Colorado requires its security practices to be within a reasonable duty of care.
    
3.  **Assessments**: Thought Machine must comply with the requirements of most states to conduct data protection assessments for certain types of processing activities, like selling or processing personal data.
    
4.  **Consumer Rights Requests**: When responding to consumer rights requests, Thought Machine must:
    
    1.  Authenticate the identity of the consumer;
        
    2.  Enable right to access data, to delete consumer-provided data, to correct inaccurate personal data, and to data portability in a non-cumbersome way;
        
    3.  Enable opt-out for sales or certain processing within 15 days; and
        
    4.  Explicitly allow an appeals process.
        
    
5.  **Processing**: Thought Machine must have data processing agreements in place, whether as a controller or processor. There is variation among the laws with respect to what should be required by the data processing agreements.
