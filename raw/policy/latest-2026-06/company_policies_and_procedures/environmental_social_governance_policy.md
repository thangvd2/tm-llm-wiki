## [](#scope "Copy link to heading")2\. Scope

### [](#thought_machine "Copy link to heading")2.1. Thought Machine

The Policy applies to all Thought Machine directors, employees and contractors for all of our activities.

### [](#thought_machine_supplier_and_partners "Copy link to heading")2.2. Thought Machine supplier and partners

Our approach to supplier and partner ESG is:

-   **Mandatory compliance:** We insist that our suppliers contractually comply with certain mandatory requirements. Usually these are legislative or regulatory requirements such as the UK Bribery Act, Modern Slavery Act, UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), etc.
    
-   **Equivalent provisions:** We require partners and selected suppliers to accept Thought Machine’s Environmental Social and Governance Policy or warrant that it has similar provisions.
    
-   **Acknowledgement:** We request partners and selected suppliers acknowledge they have read and understood certain policies, such as Thought Machine’s Code of Conduct.
    
-   **Supplier/partner specific ESG requirements:** We require our partners and suppliers to adhere to all applicable legislative and regulatory requirements, including any specific ESG requirements for their company/jurisdiction/industry. We also expect suppliers and partners to adhere to relevant Codes and Industry that are applicable.
    

## [](#environmental "Copy link to heading")3\. Environmental

Our impact on the environment includes the depletion of natural resources and contribution to climate change. We constantly need to demonstrate that sustainable options are increasingly being considered as part of day to day business. In comparison to most businesses, Thought Machine’s practices have a minimal impact on the environment. However, we believe that all businesses and individuals should be mindful of, and take action to minimise, their environmental impact regardless of size.

### [](#climate_change "Copy link to heading")3.1. Climate Change

Thought Machine is committed to working towards reducing our impact on the climate through internal initiatives and work with key suppliers.

#### [](#cloud_vs_on_premise_infrastructure_environmental_impact_and_benefits_for_customers "Copy link to heading")3.1.1. Cloud vs on-premise infrastructure: environmental impact and benefits for customers

Thought Machine builds cloud native core banking technology to replace the expensive, unreliable and inefficient legacy systems commonplace in retail banking.

Legacy systems in financial services are largely responsible for the disruption consumers face in their dayto-day banking experiences. Banks cannot serve their customers efficiently on outdated technology and there is, on average, an unplanned outage within retail banks every day. In addition, mortgage payments are known to be billed incorrectly, ATMs malfunction and customer accounts are inaccessible due to planned and unplanned outages.

These legacy IT failures have caused unacceptable harm to consumers. Thought Machine’s technology alleviates these issues by providing modern core banking technology which is highly available, resilient and scalable. This negates many of the issues of on-premise IT hardware. Our technology ensures that banks' customers have access to their accounts, the funds and services they need, and have more flexibility with the financial products they are offered.

By modernising the core of banks, consumers benefit from increased financial security, availability of services, and flexibility in their financial lives – especially in times of crisis or high demand.

On-premise legacy systems are sized for peak demand; designed to handle the highest volumes of throughput and concurrent read-write operations the bank system will expect to process. To mitigate outages caused by spikes in demand, banks must over-provision for: hardware (processing power, memory, storage, networking, etc.), infrastructure (server rooms, electricity, cabling, security, air conditioning, etc.) and human resources.

As these systems are sized for busiest periods, a large proportion of their resources are idle during a bank’s daily operations. Banks are using electricity, physical space and resources for systems which run, on average, at less than 20% utilisation. Banks can dramatically cut their costs and environmental impact by running their systems on the cloud. As cloud infrastructure providers have a large pool of customers they can smooth out peaks and operate at higher utilisation levels. Amazon Web Services (AWS) estimates that its customers are using 77% fewer servers, 84% less power and a 28% cleaner power mix than if they were running in-house data centres.

#### [](#thought_machine_consumption "Copy link to heading")3.1.2. Thought Machine consumption

Our consumption is minimal and consists of the following main areas:

-   **Office sustenance:** We strive to source these products in the most sustainable way. Practically, this means selecting products with recyclable and less packaging, whilst also considering food hygiene requirements.
    
-   **Office supplies:** As a cloud based company, Thought Machine has lower office supply needs; however, when office supplies are required, the Facilities team consider (i) products that are more durable and have longer lifespans; and (ii) the sustainability policies of suppliers.
    
-   **IT equipment:** Thought Machine considers the lifespan and energy ratings of IT equipment applying energy saving settings where possible. IT equipment is always disposed of in a responsible manner.
    
-   **Non-IT electrical equipment:** Thought Machine purchases very few items that would be considered non-IT electrical equipment, examples include fridges, shredders, etc. In purchasing this equipment, the Facilities team considers its energy ratings.
    
-   **Marketing merchandise:** The Marketing Team responsible for purchasing this merchandise prioritises quality and ethical sourcing over pricing.
    
-   **Business travel:** Thought Machine recommends that staff only travel after other feasible alternatives (e.g. video conferencing) have been considered; as a technology company, most external meetings are conducted by video conferencing.
    

#### [](#key_suppliers "Copy link to heading")3.1.3. Key suppliers

Thought Machine has identified its suppliers that have the most impact on climate change, in their delivery of products and services and we evaluate their ESG policy. These providers include:

#### [](#electric_vehicle_scheme "Copy link to heading")3.1.4. Electric Vehicle Scheme

Thought Machine has implemented a Electric Vehicle scheme via Octopus to allow our employees acquire new electric vehicles through salary sacrifice. This provides our employees the opportunity to reduce their own carbon emissions through personal use of an electric vehicle instead of petrol or diesel cars (these emissions will include commutes to and from the office).

### [](#waste_management "Copy link to heading")3.2. Waste management

Similar to other technology companies, Thought Machine generates a significant amount of electronic waste as individuals have multiple electronic devices, including desktop computers, laptops, monitors, cables, etc. Thought Machine’s approach to minimise electronic waste is to:

-   Extend asset lifecycle management, extending the usage of electronic equipment;
    
-   Consider whether electronic equipment that is not longer fit for purpose can be wiped and utilised by the local community or charities; and
    
-   Where electronic equipment is damaged or unusable, using an approved Waste Electric and Electronic Equipment (WEEE) removal company. Thought Machine is mindful about the non-electronic waste it generates and, where possible, minimises waste to landfill through various recycling reusing initiatives.
    

## [](#social "Copy link to heading")4\. Social

We have defined social as our relationship with our employees, including their rights, benefits and diversity and inclusion; clients, partners and suppliers; and community, which includes the local, national and global community. Thought Machine believes that meeting minimum legislative and regulatory requirements relating to social change is not enough and we need to actively make a positive contribution to society.

### [](#thought_machine_staff "Copy link to heading")4.1. Thought Machine Staff

Thought Machine offers various workplace rights and benefits as well as personal and social benefits that far exceed minimum requirements.

#### [](#workplace_rights_and_benefits "Copy link to heading")4.1.1. Workplace rights and benefits

Thought Machine staff have workplace rights and benefits that exceed the minimum required by regulation and legislation. All staff have the ability to develop themselves through access to mandatory and voluntary training. We believe in fair pay; therefore, we offer fair working hours, salaries that exceed the living wage, paid internships and pay benefits that far exceed the statutory minimum, including maternity and paternity leave, sick pay, compassionate leave, etc.. In addition, we have a strict code of conduct to prevent discrimination, bullying and harassment, and allow freedom of association.

#### [](#personal_benefits "Copy link to heading")4.1.2. Personal benefits

Thought Machine staff benefit from multiple personal benefits that are funded by the company, including medical insurance, mental health coaches and a cycle to work scheme for the UK headquarters.

#### [](#social_benefits "Copy link to heading")4.1.3. Social benefits

Thought Machine staff benefit from multiple funded social benefits that are funded by the company, including clubs and societies, team lunches, snacks and drinks, etc.

#### [](#diversity_and_inclusion "Copy link to heading")4.1.4. Diversity and inclusion

Thought Machine believes in the benefits of, and has taken measures to enhance, diversity and inclusion through working groups and reporting. These measures include:

-   Reporting its gender pay gap.
    
-   Three diversity groups focused on race, LGBTQ+ and Women in Technology.
    
-   A 'Women in Engineering' network.
    
-   Diversity and inclusion metrics for managers.
    
-   A wellbeing room.
    

### [](#clients_partners_and_suppliers "Copy link to heading")4.2. Clients, partners and suppliers

We have policies in place to ensure our relationship with clients, partners and suppliers make a positive difference to society or, at least, do not have a negative impact. We perform due diligence and risk assessments prior to engaging with partners and suppliers to ensure fair payment terms and not taking part in blacklisting practices.

### [](#community "Copy link to heading")4.3. Community

Thought Machine believes that it is important to contribute to the local community, including the communities and countries in which it has physical presence as well as the global and engineering community through paid volunteer days, community group projects and engineering events (eg. hackathons). We are guided by the following legislation and principles, where relevant: United Nations Universal Declaration of Human Rights Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights; International Labour Organisation (ILO); and Modern Slavery Act

## [](#governance "Copy link to heading")5\. Governance

Thought Machine’s governance framework includes: the rights and responsibilities of management; our values and codes of conduct; risk management; supply chain management; tax strategy and legal compliance. Thought Management has a robust governance structure, including a Board with non-executive directors forming the majority members and an independent Chairman; Board Committees (Audit, Remuneration and Risk); an Executive Committee; and various operational committees. The Board is responsible for holding the executive to account.

### [](#codes_of_business_conduct "Copy link to heading")5.1. Codes of business conduct

Thought Machine has a group wide Code of Conduct, which is applicable to all employees regardless of employment agreement as well as contractors, Board members, and anyone acting on behalf of Thought Machine. The Code of Conduct has sections on: our culture; respecting each other; workplace safety; conflicts of interest; gifts and hospitality; protecting and maintaining confidentiality; protecting assets; compliance with legal regulatory and contractual obligations; fraud; whistle-blowing; protecting our reputation; and financial and social responsibility. Some of the most pertinent aspects of the Code related to ESG are:

-   **Bribery & Corruption:** Thought Machine takes a zero-tolerance approach to bribery and corruption and is committed to acting professionally, fairly and with integrity in all business dealings and relationships.
    
-   **Competition:** Thought Machine respects its competitors. We want to compete with them fairly and in accordance with competition laws.
    

### [](#risk_management "Copy link to heading")5.2. Risk management

Thought Management manages its risks through a dedicated risk function. Periodic risk assessments are undertaken; risks are identified, quantified; and risk treatment plans are maintained. Risks are escalated and governed by operational committees, the Executive Committee and the Board.

### [](#health_and_safety "Copy link to heading")5.3. Health and Safety

Thought Machine takes its health and safety responsibilities seriously and, although the health and safety risks related to our business activities are low, our facilities team ensures that all reasonable precautions are taken to protect the health and safety of our employees. Health and safety training is provided on induction, we maintain a health and safety policy and have trained fire wardens and first aiders in all our offices. We also have dedicated sections for Health and Safety on Facilities intranet pages.

### [](#supply_chain_management "Copy link to heading")5.4. Supply chain management

Thought Machine has a robust approach to Third Party Risk Management (TPRM), which includes performing due diligence prior to engaging third parties and on an on-going basis, depending on the risk of the supplier. Suppliers that are not aligned with ESG Principles for Third Parties, Suppliers and Partners may be promptly off-boarded. In addition, Thought Machine has a legal and moral duty to report any illegal behaviour undertaken by its suppliers to relevant authorities.

## [](#targets_and_assessments "Copy link to heading")6\. Targets and assessments

Thought Machine’s long term plans are to set targets and perform ESG assessments; however, both of these activities would have greater value when we manage at least one our own buildings, allowing us to make practical changes to its activities. Thought Machine’s approach to targets and assessments are set out in this section.

### [](#targets "Copy link to heading")6.1. Targets

We are looking to develop a consistent approach to measure our environmental impact and carbon footprint before setting targets. Generally, Thought Machine aspires to: Reduce energy consumption (per person); Work with its building providers to use renewable energy sources; Use sustainable suppliers; Minimise business travel; and Minimise waste generated, and ensure it is disposed of in the least harmful manner.

### [](#assessments "Copy link to heading")6.2. Assessments

Thought Machine believes that ESG and sustainability assessments should be relevant for the industry / sector in which they operate. Thought Machine is looking at suitable providers to undertake an ESG assessment.
