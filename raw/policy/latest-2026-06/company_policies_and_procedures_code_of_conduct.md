## [](#introduction "Copy link to heading")1\. Introduction

The Thought Machine Code of Conduct describes our expectations regarding employees’ behaviour towards their colleagues, managers, clients and other stakeholders. Our Code of Conduct is built around our desire for excellence. We set high standards because it enables us to build amazing products and attract great people to work here. We promote freedom of expression and open communication because we believe that’s how we will get the best outcomes for us and our clients; this should also enable everyone to challenge behaviour that we deem to be unacceptable. Everything we do is measured against the highest standards, be they quality, innovation or ethical standards.

Our code of conduct encompasses elements of our policies related to the workplace, including:

-   The behaviour expected from individuals who work at Thought Machine;
    
-   Requirements to ensure the safety of our employees and clients;
    
-   Legal and regulatory requirements applicable to Thought Machine; and
    
-   Client contractual obligations that are applicable to individuals.
    

We also expect all employees to follow our Code of Conduct. They should avoid offending, participating in serious disputes and disrupting our workplace. We also expect them to foster a well-organised, respectful and collaborative work environment. Please do read the Code of Conduct and follow this in both spirit and letter and encourage others to incorporate the principles of the Code into our work.

### [](#who_must_follow_the_code "Copy link to heading")1.1. Who must follow the Code?

This Code of Conduct applies to all our employees regardless of the employment agreement. It also applies to contractors, Board members, and anyone acting on behalf of Thought Machine. We expect our contractors, consultants, and others who may be temporarily assigned to perform work or services for Thought Machine to follow the Code in connection with their work for us. Failure to follow this code can result in disciplinary action, including termination of employment or termination of our relationship with contractors, consultants, or other covered service providers.

### [](#overall_code_of_conduct "Copy link to heading")1.2. Overall Code of Conduct

We are committed to a supportive work environment, where employees have the opportunity to reach their highest potential. We expect our employees to do their utmost to create a workplace culture that is free of harassment, intimidation, bias, and unlawful discrimination and follows these six basic principles:

1.  Respect each other, including clients and suppliers;
    
2.  Avoid conflicts of interest;
    
3.  Protect and maintain confidentiality;
    
4.  Protect our assets;
    
5.  Comply with our legal, regulatory and contractual obligations; and finally;
    
6.  Be financially and socially responsible.
    

## [](#our_culture "Copy link to heading")2\. Our Culture

We have, and continue to, put a lot of effort in creating a culture at Thought Machine that attracts and retains the best people and one that encourages our most creative work. It starts with *“fit for our culture”* coming first and foremost in our hiring process.

One of our founding beliefs is that our success has been, and will continue to be, in no small part due to our *one-team* ethos. Our lunch roulettes, TMF, regular gatherings and socials go a long way to maintaining good relationships across and within teams. These ward against silos or protectionist behaviours. In terms of how we work, we think and act in collaborative and respectful ways, extending a helping hand when others need it. We show interest in teams beyond our own because we recognise that everyone in the organisation is an important and essential part of our company. Whilst individuals have different responsibilities, we respect each other’s views and contributions and acknowledge that no matter the role, we are stronger collectively.

*Engineering excellence* is something we work hard to deliver. We strive to produce the best products that will exceed customer expectations. We know when we need to put in extra effort and are happy to go the extra mile. This is not only about hard work and achieving excellence but also means having technical rigour in our ways of working, our policies and our processes. This isn’t just a job for Thought Machiners - it is a calling.

In making decisions and creating processes, we subscribe to **debate and commit** approach. We actively listen to others and encourage different views to be aired in a candid, respectful and constructive way. We seek to ensure that the pros and cons are weighed up and then those responsible for the team or business area make the decision. We all get behind them, whatever our views and we put our efforts into making it work. We do this because we firmly believe in getting on with the task at hand once the decision has been made. We recognise that approaches will need to evolve and change and when a process is not working for us anymore, we review using the **debate and commit** approach to find better ways of doing things.

We *trust* our employees and managers to take *responsibility* and when there isn’t a precedent to guide them, we know they will make good judgements. This is because every single Thought Machiner is an intelligent, capable and resourceful individual. When mistakes (not if, because we have the *humility* to know that greatness isn’t achieved without making wrong turns) are made or when things do not go to plan, we own up to it and we take *responsibility* to fix the situation. We always take time to conduct a retrospective and look to learn from the opportunity by sharing this learning with others.

*Openness* is a key strength of ours. Everyone is approachable. Our Executive Team meets with the whole business every week to proactively share what is going on across the company, as well as being open to questions from the team. Additionally, peer reviews are conducted across the business. This environment requires our team to be egoless and accept the thoughts and views of others irrespective of their job title. Thought Machiners are not perturbed by this constant critique, instead we embrace the opportunity to continuously learn and improve.

Often our decisions and reviews are conducted online, this makes it *open* to all, fully transparent and creates a permanency to it. By conducting reviews online, everyone who wants or needs to contribute can do so at a level that is appropriate for their knowledge and experience. We also recognise that transparency can cause lots of noise and distraction, so we encourage quiet reflection rather than gut judgement. We believe this gets a better response when reviewing or inventing. Face to face discussions of course has their place, sometimes for cracking a tough problem, or for when people can’t agree.

This *openness* extends to seeking the truth. In any organisation, communication can get distorted, lost in translation or not reach everyone that should receive it. Thought Machiners know this, so should never engage in gossip and, when unsure, they should always take a step back and seek the direct path to the truth.

We also know we have to *balance* hard-work, tenacity and determination so that our efforts are sustainable for the long-term. We look out for each other, ensuring we are all “ok”. To maintain our personal resilience, we make time for the non-urgent and non-work things that are important to us. Clubs, sports and social activities are encouraged and organised for Thought Machiners. This is because we recognise that we all need to relax, blow off steam and maintain a *balance* in our lives.

*Our position on political and social matters*

At Thought Machine, we do not comment on social or political issues. However, we may, at times, make public statements on government policy that directly affects the technology industry and our business operations––such as corporate tax or employment legislation.

That said, we want to reiterate we are committed to creating an inclusive, respectful, and supportive workplace and oppose all forms of discrimination.

### [](#harassment "Copy link to heading")3.2. Harassment

Thought Machine believes that the working environment should at all times be supportive of the dignity and respect of individuals. If a complaint of harassment is brought to the attention of Management, it will be investigated promptly and appropriate action will be taken.

#### [](#what_is_harassment "Copy link to heading")3.2.1. What is harassment?

Harassment can be defined as conduct, which is unwanted and offensive and affects the dignity of an individual or group of individuals.

Sexual harassment is defined as *“unwanted conduct of a sexual nature, or other conduct based on sex, affecting the dignity of women and men at work”*. This can include unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct.

People can be subject to harassment on a wide variety of grounds including:

-   race, ethnic origin, nationality or skin colour
    
-   sex or sexual orientation
    
-   religious or political convictions
    
-   willingness to challenge harassment, leading to victimisation
    
-   disabilities, sensory impairments or learning difficulties
    
-   status as ex-offenders
    
-   age
    
-   real or suspected infection with a blood-borne virus (e.g. AIDS/HIV)
    

Forms may include:

-   physical contact ranging from touching to serious assault
    
-   verbal and written harassment through jokes, offensive language, gossip and slander, sectarian songs, letters, etc.
    
-   visual display of posters, graffiti, obscene gestures, flags and emblems
    
-   isolation or non-cooperation at work, exclusion from social activities
    
-   coercion ranging from pressure for sexual favours to pressure to participate in political/religious groups
    
-   intrusion by pestering, spying, following someone
    
-   bullying
    

#### [](#what_should_i_do_if_i_am_subject_to_harassment "Copy link to heading")3.2.2. What should I do if I am subject to harassment?

If you feel you are being harassed you are strongly encouraged to seek early advice/support from your line manager. If you feel your line manager is harassing you, then you should contact HR.

You should also keep a written record detailing the incidents of harassment and any requests made to the harasser to stop. This written record should be made as soon as possible after the events giving rise to concern and should include dates, times, places and the circumstances of what happened.

Thought Machine has a formal procedure for dealing with these issues which you can find in the handbook or by speaking to a member of the HR team. In the handbook, you will also understand the consequences for violating the policy, the process for raising concerns, procedures for a timely and confidential investigation of a complaint, how to make a report of suspected harassment, retaliation and internal resources employees can speak with if they have questions about the whole process.

## [](#avoid_conflicts_of_interest "Copy link to heading")4\. Avoid Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest is defined as any actual or perceived situation where an individual’s actions could lead to:

-   **a personal conflict:** a benefit for you, your friends, or your family at the expense of Thought Machine or our clients; or
    
-   **a client conflict:** the interests of one client are prioritised at the expense of the interests of another client.
    

All of us should avoid personal conflicts of interest and circumstances that reasonably present the appearance of a conflict. However, client conflicts can often not be avoided and therefore they need to be appropriately managed.

When considering a course of action related to a personal conflict, ask yourself whether the action you’re considering could create an incentive for you, or appear to others to create an incentive for you, to benefit yourself, your friends or family, or an associated business at the expense of Thought Machine or our clients. If the answer is “yes,” the action you’re considering is likely to create a conflict of interest situation, and you should avoid it.

Some personal conflicts of interest are unavoidable, in these circumstances you should always discuss the matter firstly with your manager and then log it in the Conflict of Interest Register, this will then be reviewed by the management team who will confirm to you what action is appropriate to take.

Client conflicts need to be carefully managed by the business as work may need to be prioritised to meet business needs or contractual requirements. Identified client conflicts should be first discussed with your manager and then if required, escalated to the Head of Operations.

### [](#investments_advisory_roles_or_business_ventures "Copy link to heading")4.1. Investments, advisory roles or business ventures

During your employment, you must not without the prior written approval of HR, either on your own or jointly with, for or on behalf of any other person or entity, directly or indirectly, set up, carry out or be employed, engaged or interested in any business, trade or occupation (including public, private or charitable work) other than that of Thought Machine.

However, you are permitted to own up to 5% of a company’s issued shares for investment purposes whether or not the company is listed on a stock exchange.

Should you be in doubt as to whether an activity involves a conflict, or you identify that a conflict has occurred you should discuss the situation with your manager or the Chief People Officer to seek clarity on the appropriate actions for you to take.

If you have identified an actual or potential conflict of interest you log it in the Conflict of Interest Register this will then be reviewed by the management team and will confirm whether or not you can continue with the activity or not.

### [](#gifts_and_hospitality "Copy link to heading")4.2. Gifts and Hospitality

Your working relationships may bring you into contact with outside organisations where it is normal business practice or social convention to offer or receive hospitality, and sometimes gifts. Offers of this kind to you or your family can lead to a conflict of interest. Therefore, no employee or any member of his or her immediate family should accept, from a supplier, client or other person doing business with Thought Machine (this does not include lunches, dinners or gifts paid for by the Company), payments of money under any circumstances or special considerations, such as discounts or gifts of materials, equipment, services, facilities or anything else of value unless they are in each instance of a very minor nature usually associated with accepted business practice. Any gift, entertainment, hospitality or sponsorship with a value over £100 per person must be requested and approved by must be requested and approved by logging it in the Gifts and Hospitality Register. This will then go onto the Gifts, Hospitality and Sponsorship register held by HR and compliance.

## [](#protect_and_maintain_confidentiality "Copy link to heading")5\. Protect and Maintain Confidentiality

During the course of your employment, you may find yourself in possession of sensitive information, the disclosure of which could be construed as a breach of confidentiality. This information may be confidential to Thought Machine, our clients or our employees. If confidential information is inappropriately shared, it could have a number of consequences, which include: damaging our competitive advantage, contractual breach, impacting our marketing efforts and causing reputational damage.

### [](#confidentiality "Copy link to heading")5.1. Confidentiality

It is a condition of your employment that you have a duty of confidentiality to Thought Machine, and you must not discuss any company sensitive, client sensitive or confidential information whatsoever with any outside organisation including the media. The Thought Machine Data Protection Policy covers this subject in more detail.

Any deliberate breach of confidentiality would be deemed as gross misconduct except as otherwise provided or as permitted by any current legislation (e.g. the UK Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998) and could lead to your dismissal.

Pictures which you and your guests take of information written on whiteboards or of documents etc or such photos that are shared may contain confidential information. It is up to you to be sure that those pictures don’t disclose confidential information.

Some of us will find ourselves having family or other personal relationships with people employed by our competitors or business partners. As in most cases, common sense applies. Don’t tell your significant other or family members anything confidential, and don’t solicit confidential information from them about their company, don’t engage in gossip.

There are “grey areas” in which you will need to apply your best judgment in making sure you don’t disclose any confidential information; it is recommended you be cautious and ask for guidance from Risk & Compliance.

### [](#competitorsformer_employers "Copy link to heading")5.2. Competitors/former employers

We respect our competitors. We want to compete with them fairly and we don’t want their confidential information. The same goes for confidential information belonging to any of Thought Machine’s former employers. If an opportunity arises to take advantage of a competitor’s or former employer’s confidential information, don’t do it (see section 7 .3 on anti-competitive behaviour).

### [](#clientspartnerships "Copy link to heading")5.3. Clients/partnerships

Just as you are careful not to disclose confidential Thought Machine information, it’s equally important not to disclose any confidential information from our clients or partners. Don’t accept confidential information from other companies without first having all parties sign an appropriate Non-disclosure Agreement approved by Legal. Even after the agreement is signed, try only to accept as much information as you need to accomplish your business objectives.

## [](#protect_our_assets "Copy link to heading")6\. Protect our assets

Thought Machine has two key types of assets, physical assets (laptops, servers, etc) and intellectual property. All of us are responsible for protecting both of these types of assets.

### [](#our_physical_assets "Copy link to heading")6.1. Our physical assets

Thought Machine invests a significant amount of money to ensure its employees have the tools and equipment to perform their roles; this includes furniture, computer equipment and other equipment (e.g. fridges, coffee machines, etc.). Although Thought Machine may not own all of its equipment, it is responsible for the costs related to replacing and repairing assets. It is the responsibility of all employees to treat Thought Machine assets with due care to minimise damage, loss or theft.

### [](#our_intellectual_property "Copy link to heading")6.2. Our intellectual property

As an engineering company, intellectual property is one of our most important assets. Whilst there are controls in place to protect our intellectual property, all employees need to be cautious when sharing any information related to our products, particularly when sharing access or documentation with externals. If anyone is unsure about the information they are sharing, they should consult their line manager, Security or Compliance.

Claims to intellectual property often lead to long and expensive legal battles. Thought Machine can take steps to mitigate these battles by ensuring only Thought Machine employees are involved in developing our products and, where external assistance is required, having clear contractual terms that protect our intellectual property. If you plan to work with any third party to develop our product, including our Code, please seek advice from Compliance or Legal.

#### [](#inventions "Copy link to heading")6.2.1. Inventions

Developing or helping to develop outside inventions that relate to Thought Machine’s existing or reasonably expected products and services, relate to your role at Thought Machine or are developed using Thought Machine’s resources may create conflicts of interest and be subject to the provisions set out in your employment contract. If you have any questions about potential conflicts or intellectual property ownership involving an outside invention or other intellectual property consult with the Legal Team.

## [](#comply_with_our_legal_regulatory_and_contractual_obligations "Copy link to heading")7\. Comply with our legal, regulatory and contractual obligations

Thought Machine is required to comply with various legislation and regulations in the UK and abroad. In this section, we have included group-wide requirements that require the support of all employees to ensure compliance.

### [](#anti_slavery_people_trafficking "Copy link to heading")7.1. Anti-Slavery & People Trafficking

We have a zero-tolerance policy to modern slavery and we are committed to acting ethically and with integrity in all our business dealings and relationships and to implementing and enforcing effective systems and controls to ensure modern slavery is not taking place anywhere in our own business or in any of our supply chains.

We expect the same high standards from all of our contractors, suppliers and other business partners, and as part of our contracting processes, we include specific prohibitions against the use of forced, compulsory or trafficked labour, or anyone held in slavery or servitude, whether adults or children, and we expect that our suppliers will hold their own suppliers to the same high standards.

If you are investigated, charged or prosecuted for any anti-slavery or people trafficking crimes anywhere in the world, you must report it to Compliance, regardless of whether you are convicted.

### [](#anti_bribery_corruption "Copy link to heading")7.2. Anti-Bribery & Corruption

As a global company, Thought Machine is bound by local and international laws, including the *UK Bribery Act 2010, US Foreign Corruptions Practices Act (FCPA) 1977 and Singapore Prevention of Corruption Act 1960*. It is our policy is to ensure that Thought Machine conducts its business in an honest and ethical manner reflecting the highest standards of integrity and in compliance with all relevant bribery and corruption legislation and regulations applicable. We take a zero-tolerance approach to bribery and corruption and are committed to acting professionally, fairly and with integrity in all our business dealings and relationships. Please see the Anti-Bribery and Corruption Policy for further details.

### [](#anti_competitive_behaviour "Copy link to heading")7.3. Anti-competitive behaviour

As a company and as individuals we are obliged by law to comply with various rules at the national and European levels that are designed to ensure that competition between businesses within the UK and Europe is not unduly restricted. Competition compliance is all about upholding these rules and more importantly about being seen to uphold these rules.

Engaging with competitors, including former colleagues, and obtaining non-public information about competitors may lead to Thought Machine being investigated for anti-competitive behaviour. Whether an arrangement is anti-competitive is assessed on the basis of its objective, or its effect on competition, rather than its wording or form. This means that verbal and informal 'gentlemen’s agreements' are equally capable of being found to be anti-competitive as formal, written agreements.

Thought Machines are committed to always act fairly and in accordance with competition laws. Any breach of this policy may be considered serious misconduct. If you believe that you, or a colleague, may have been involved in anti-competitive behaviour, you must report it to Legal or Compliance immediately.

### [](#fraud "Copy link to heading")7.4. Fraud

Our fraud policy applies to any irregularity, or suspected irregularity, involving employees as well as consultants, suppliers, contractors, and/or any other parties with a business relationship with this organisation. Any investigative activity required will be conducted without regard to any person’s relationship to this organisation, position or length of service.

Thought Machine has procedures in place that reduce the likelihood of fraud occurring. These include documented procedures and documented systems of internal control and risk assessment. In addition, the board tries to ensure that a risk and fraud awareness culture exists in this organisation.

#### [](#actions_constituting_fraud "Copy link to heading")7.4.1. Actions Constituting Fraud

‘Fraud’ at Thought Machine is defined as obtaining or taking money or benefits in the means of deception when the money or benefits are not entitled to a person, this includes whether the person had attempted to take or obtain money or benefits. Fraudulent activities consist of being dishonest or deceitful with the intent to obtain or continue to receive financial advantage or benefit for themselves or another person whether tangible or not, intentional misrepresentations affecting the financial statements by one or more individuals among management, staff or third parties.

Thought Machine has a zero-tolerance for any form of financial crime or fraud under any circumstances.

All managers and supervisors have a duty to familiarise themselves with the types of improprieties that might be expected to occur within their areas of responsibility and to be alert for any indications of irregularity.

The Board is absolutely committed to maintaining an honest, open and well-intentioned atmosphere within the organisation. It is, therefore, also committed to the elimination of any fraud within the organisation, and to the rigorous investigation of any such cases.

The Board wishes to encourage anyone having reasonable suspicions of fraud to report them. Therefore, it is also the board’s policy, which will be rigorously enforced, that no employee will suffer in any way as a result of reporting reasonably held suspicions of fraud. For these purposes ‘reasonably held suspicions’ shall mean any suspicions other than those which are shown to be raised maliciously and found to be groundless. The organisation will deal with all occurrences in accordance with the Public Interest Disclosure Act.

Generally, managers and supervisors are in a position to take responsibility for detecting fraud and other irregularities in their area. Staff must assist management by reporting any suspected irregularities.

-   Do not try to investigate the fraud personally
    
-   Do not confront the individual that you suspect
    
-   Do not talk to anyone else about your suspicions
    

In most cases, staff should report concerns of impropriety in line with the Whistle-blowing Policy (section 7.5 below). However, where the concern is considered to constitute fraud as defined above, staff should inform the Finance Director or CFO.

#### [](#fraud_investigation_and_reporting "Copy link to heading")7.4.2. Fraud Investigation and Reporting

The CFO has overall responsibility for Thought Machine’s response to fraud. The Finance Director has responsibility for coordinating any investigation and for keeping the fraud response plan up to date. The Finance Director and CFO hold the master copy of the fraud investigation log and response plan and are responsible for managing any investigation. The investigations log documents all reported suspicions, including those dismissed as minor or otherwise not investigated. The log will contain details of actions taken and conclusions reached and is used as an important tool for managing, reporting and evaluating lessons learned.

If a suspicion of fraud involves the Finance Director or an Executive Director, the matter should be reported directly to the Chairman of the Audit Committee.

### [](#whistle_blowing "Copy link to heading")7.5. Whistle-blowing

Thought Machine is committed to conducting its business with honesty and integrity, and we expect all staff to maintain high standards. All organisations, however, face the risk of things going wrong from time to time, or of unknowingly harbouring illegal or unethical conduct. A culture of openness and accountability is essential in order to prevent such situations from occurring or to address them when they do occur.

Whistleblowing is the disclosure of information which relates to suspected wrongdoing or dangers at work. This may include:

-   criminal activity;
    
-   miscarriages of justice;
    
-   suspected fraud by staff, contractors, or our third-party suppliers;
    
-   danger to health and safety;
    
-   damage to the environment;
    
-   failure to comply with any legal obligation or regulatory requirements;
    
-   the deliberate concealment of any of the above matters.
    

A whistleblower is a person who raises a genuine concern in good faith relating to any of the above. If you have any genuine concerns related to suspected wrongdoing or danger affecting any of our activities or those of our clients (a whistleblowing concern) you should report it under this policy.

In the first instance, we would recommend that you raise any concerns with your line manager. In some cases they may refer the matter to the Chief People Officer. If you do not feel comfortable raising your concern with your line manager you have the option of contacting HR directly. Management inaction of known issues will not be tolerated and will be subject to formal action under the applicable disciplinary process.

The Chief People Officer will arrange a meeting with you as soon as possible to discuss your concern. You may bring a colleague to any meetings under this policy. Your companion must respect the confidentiality of your disclosure and any subsequent investigation. We will take down a summary of your concern and provide you with a copy after the meeting. We will also aim to give you an indication of how we propose to deal with the matter.

Whistleblowers who are concerned about possible reprisals if their identity is revealed should come forward to the Chief People Officer and appropriate measures can then be taken to preserve confidentiality. We will make every effort to keep your identity secret. If it is necessary for anyone investigating your concern to know your identity, we will discuss this with you before doing so.

Once you have raised a concern, we will carry out an initial assessment to determine the scope of any investigation. We will inform you of the outcome of our assessment. You may be required to attend additional meetings in order to provide further information. In some cases we may appoint an investigator or team of investigators including staff with relevant experience of investigations or specialist knowledge of the subject matter. The investigator(s) may make recommendations for change to enable us to minimise the risk of future wrongdoing.

We will keep you informed of the progress of the investigation and its likely timescale. However, sometimes the need for confidentiality may prevent us from giving you specific details of the investigation or any disciplinary action taken as a result. You should treat any information about the investigation as confidential.

While we cannot always guarantee the outcome you are seeking, we will try to deal with your concerns fairly and in an appropriate way. By using this policy you can help us to achieve this.

If you are not happy with the way in which your concern has been handled, you can raise it with the Chief People Officer.

It is understandable that whistleblowers are sometimes worried about possible repercussions. We aim to encourage openness and will support staff who raise genuine concerns in good faith under this policy, even if they turn out to be mistaken. Employees must not suffer any detrimental treatment as a result of raising a concern in good faith. Detrimental treatment includes dismissal, disciplinary action, threats or other unfavourable treatment connected with raising a concern. If you believe that you have suffered any such treatment, you should inform the Chief People Officer immediately.

### [](#external_disclosures "Copy link to heading")7.6. External disclosures

The law recognises that in some circumstances it may be appropriate for you to report your concerns to someone other than your employer. The UK Government list prescribed people and bodies you can report malpractice, other than your employer. If you decide to blow the whistle on a prescribed person rather than your employer, you must make sure that you have chosen the correct person or body for your issue. We strongly encourage you to seek advice before reporting a concern to anyone external and to only use this process for very serious known malpractice matters.

## [](#protecting_our_reputation "Copy link to heading")8\. Protecting our reputation

We pride ourselves on our culture of generosity and openness within the company. Our ability to continue these practices depends on us all considering the reputation of the company in our actions each day.

### [](#communications "Copy link to heading")8.1. Communications

We recognise that the use of email and other communication tools including (but not restricted to) Slack, and Phabricator can help our employees to work more productively and enable effective collaboration. However, communicating on such mediums does require care and thought and can pose some risks. Written communication is permanent and can lack the immediate feedback that you receive in face-to-face interactions and it can be less easy to gauge whether you have been understood or even caused offence. We have therefore listed below acceptable usage of communications tools at Thought Machine.

-   You must not create or forward any content that could be considered to be offensive, harassing, derogatory (including to our people, customers or competitors), discriminatory, illegal or pornographic. Banter and jokes, whilst often not intending to cause offence, can be received differently from the humour that was intended. Colleagues will have differing views and feelings and differing levels of sensitivity about certain matters. Furthermore, no individual employee can ever know everything about his or her colleagues. In line with our approach to harassment, we require all employees to treat their colleagues with dignity and respect and refrain from any behaviour that might cause offence, whether that be in person, in written communication or when using social communication tools.
    
-   You must not swear or comment in a derogatory way when writing reviews or commenting in Phabricator (or other mediums). You should stick to objective facts, as this is a far more constructive approach. These comments can be seen by clients and such language is likely to harm the reputation of our business.
    
-   It is important to take great care when drafting communications, in particular, what is said and how views are expressed. You should try to make sure that the content of business emails is relevant and carefully worded, keep to facts and avoid commentary that criticises our competitors or their employees. You should also avoid statements that suggest that there are quality problems with goods or services of suppliers or customers unless the communication is made directly with those in the partnering organisation who are working with us to resolve such issues, and those stating that anyone is incompetent.
    
-   At Thought Machine we are tolerant and accepting of our employees who have a wide range of views on social issues, religious views and across the political spectrum. We expect all our employees to demonstrate the same high level of tolerance. It is not acceptable to be overly critical of another employee’s views and make them feel uncomfortable or feel excluded from the company for holding them. To that end, do not assume that your views on these subjects are the only views, they are correct, or that there must be something wrong with people who hold different views. To uphold this we ask all employees to refrain from entering into antagonistic arguments using a work email, Slack or other channels to discuss such issues or petition for causes. We have seen in the past that when used for these topics it has caused considerable unhappiness and has the potential to be a direct infringement of the harassment policy.
    
-   It is important to remember that internal communications can, on occasions, be legally binding documents and their disclosure can be required in legal proceedings. If you are unsure of the legal consequences of communication, please check with a member of the people or Leadership team.
    
-   You must not manipulate any communications for unauthorised purposes (e.g. forging of date or sender address).
    
-   Tools like Slack should not be used as a place to moan especially in forums which can be seen by either the entire company or entire regions(General, Announcements, Location-London, Location-USA etc.). If you have an issue or concern about a company decision then reach out to the business leader or the people team.
    
-   Do not share outside the company any non-public documentation. Consult your manager or the Risk & Compliance team if in doubt
    
-   Do not provide non-Thought Machine employees access to our internal data repositories such as Confluence, Google Drive, or other data repositories.
    

### [](#using_social_media "Copy link to heading")8.2. Using social media

Thought Machine recognises that many employees make use of social media in a personal capacity. While they are not acting on behalf of the organisation, employees must be aware that they have the potential to damage the organisation if they are recognised as being one of our employees.

Employees are allowed to say that they work for the organisation, which recognises that it is natural for its staff sometimes to want to discuss their work on social media. However, the employee’s social media profile must contain a disclaimer which makes clear that the individual is publishing in a personal capacity and not on behalf of the business. Suggestions include:

-   “Views are my own and not those of my employer”
    
-   “All views expressed are my own”
    

Any communications that employees make in a personal capacity through social media must not:

-   make any reference to clients or the work with clients unless you have had explicit permission from Compliance, Legal, or Communications;
    
-   bring the organisation or any of its clients into disrepute, for example by:
    
    -   criticising or arguing with customers, colleagues or rivals;
        
    -   making defamatory comments about individuals or other organisations or groups; or
        
    -   posting images that are inappropriate or links to inappropriate content;
        
    
-   breach confidentiality, for example by:
    
    -   revealing trade secrets or information owned by the organisation;
        
    -   giving away confidential information about an individual (such as a colleague or customer contact) or organisation (such as a rival business); or
        
    -   discussing the organisation’s internal workings (such as deals that it is doing with a client, proprietary technology in use, or its future business plans that have not been communicated to the public);
        
    
-   breach copyright, for example by:
    
    -   using someone else’s images, or written content without permission, this includes references to our clients and the logos of our clients;
        
    -   failing to give acknowledgement where permission has been given to reproduce something; or
        
    
-   do anything that could be considered discriminatory against, or bullying or harassment of any individual, for example by:
    
    -   making offensive or derogatory comments relating to sex, gender reassignment, race (including nationality), disability, sexual orientation, religion or belief or age;
        
    -   using social media to bully another individual (such as an employee of the organisation); or
        
    -   posting images that are discriminatory or offensive or links to such content.
        
    

All Thought Machine employees have been invited to use an approved tool, Advocacy, to make social media management easier.

-   Employees must adhere to the above code of conduct in all instances of using social media, including with Bambu
    

If you observe something which you feel is a breach of the policy above, you should notify a member of the Compliance team immediately by logging it in the Social Media Breaches Register.

The Compliance team will retain records of all breaches of social media use policy reports, incidents and escalations.

## [](#financially_and_socially_responsible "Copy link to heading")9\. Financially and socially responsible

Financial integrity and fiscal responsibility are core aspects of any successful business. The money we spend is not ours; it’s the company’s and, ultimately, our shareholders. Every one of us has a role in making sure that money is spent appropriately, our financial records are accurate and timely, and internal processes are followed.

### [](#spending_money_wisely "Copy link to heading")9.1. Spending money wisely

When you spend money whilst on Thought Machine business, make sure that the cost is reasonable, directly related to company business, supported by appropriate documentation and adheres to our purchasing and procurement policy and travel and expenses policy. Always record the business purpose (e.g., if you take someone out to dinner, always record the full names and titles of the people who attended as well as the reason for the dinner) and comply with other submission requirements. Thought Machine employees are not permitted to use any company fund for gambling. If you’re uncertain about whether you should spend money or submit an expense for reimbursement, check with your manager. Managers are responsible for all money spent and expenses incurred by their direct reports, and should carefully review such spending and expenses before approving. Expense claims should be submitted to [finance@thoughtmachine.net](mailto:finance@thoughtmachine.net).

Thought Machine is committed to high legal, ethical and moral standards. All employees are expected to share this commitment. We have procedures in place that reduce the likelihood of fraud or tax evasion occurring. We encourage anyone having reasonable suspicions of fraud to report them as outlined in the whistle-blowing section of this policy.

### [](#entering_into_partnerships_and_contracts "Copy link to heading")9.2. Entering into partnerships and contracts

Never sign any contract on behalf of Thought Machine unless; you are authorised to do so (if you are unsure whether you are authorised, ask your manager) and the contract has been approved by Legal, Finance and Compliance (this includes agreeing to online terms and conditions). This ensures that we get the best possible deal for Thought Machine as whilst price is very important, it isn’t the only factor worth considering. Quality, service, reliability, risk of losing intellectual property and the terms and conditions of the proposed deal may also affect the final decision.

### [](#environmental_social_governanceesg "Copy link to heading")9.3. Environmental, Social, Governance(ESG)

Thought Machine recognises that the purpose of a company should not solely be profit and its activities have an impact on the wider world; therefore it is committed to making a positive difference for the environment, our stakeholders (including our employees), the community and the wider world.

At a high level, we are fundamentally committed to building a long-term, sustainable business, which will grow, provide employment and generate economic benefit in an environmentally and socially responsible manner. To achieve this, we must:

-   Be mindful of the impact of our decisions on clients, colleagues and communities.
    
-   Manage our resources in a way that ensures we have a positive impact on society and also minimise our impact on the environment.
    
-   Be proactive in the way we meet, or aim to exceed, legal and regulatory requirements.
    
-   Conduct business in an ethical way, in line with our corporate values.
    

As well as monitoring its own ESG impacts, Thought Machine considers the impact of its suppliers; assessing a supplier’s ethos to ESG is assessed prior to contracting and on an ongoing basis.

Thought Machine’s approach to ESG is outlined in the Environmental, Social and Governance Policy.

#### [](#environmental_policy "Copy link to heading")9.3.1. Environmental Policy

Thought Machine recognises that it has a responsibility to the environment beyond legal and regulatory requirements. We are committed to reducing our environmental impact where possible, and continually improving our environmental performance as an integral part of our business strategy and operating methods, with regular review points. Employees are expected to find ways to support this ethos; being proactive in reducing waste and minimising the impact on the environment whilst fulfilling their duties at Thought Machine.

### [](#political_and_charitable_giving "Copy link to heading")9.4. Political and charitable giving

Using Thought Machine funds for all political and charitable donations over £10,000 require Board sign off and all charitable donations under £10,000 require sign off from the Chief Financial Officer(CFO); therefore, no employee is permitted to use any company money for political or charitable donation without explicit sign-off. This includes spending money on political and charitable dinners and auctions.

Overall, Thought Machine supports charitable giving as long as it is aligned with this Code of Conduct. We actively participate in initiatives ranging from supporting charities, Women in Tech and Engineering, and Women Who Code(WWC). We also promote STEM Learning as well as physical endeavours with charity running.

On occasions, clients/partners or suppliers may request either in a personal or professional capacity donations to a charity event or activity. It is company policy to not contribute to such requests. The following text may be used in a response to such a request:

*'It is our company policy to neither solicit nor make contributions or charitable requests to or from clients, suppliers or partners, this does not mean we do not value or contribute to charity, Thought Machine under takes various charitable activities through out they earand we are proud of our social responsibilities.'*

Thought Machine Group Limited, employees and contractors should also not make a request to a client, partner or supplier for charitable contributions in either a personal or professional capacity.

Nothing in this policy prevents employees from using their own money for political or charitable donations or requesting personal donations from colleagues.

## [](#speak_up "Copy link to heading")10\. Speak Up

Thought Machine employees have the right to raise their concerns to the Chief People Officer, as outlined in the WhistleBlowing Section of the Code of Conduct, Bullying and Harassment and Grievance Policies.

Upon receival of a concern, the Chief People Officer will carry out an initial assessment to determine the scope of any required investigation. They will then arrange a meeting with the employee as soon as possible to discuss their concern. The employee may bring a colleague to any meetings under this policy. However, the companion must respect the confidentiality of the disclosure and any subsequent investigation. The Chief People Officer will take down a summary of the concern and provide the employee with a copy after the meeting. The Chief People Officer will also aim to give an indication of how they propose to deal with the matter. All details of the investigation will be kept confidential and stored in the employee’s Human Resources file with restricted access. If the Chief People Officer requires to discuss the concern with the perpetrator, then all personal information will be redacted unless consent is given by the employee raising the concern.

The employee raising the concern will be kept informed of the investigation’s progress and its likely timescale. However, sometimes the need for confidentiality may prevent giving specific details of the investigation or any disciplinary action taken as a result. The employee should treat any information about the investigation as confidential. It is understandable that employees raising grievance or whistleblowers are sometimes worried about possible repercussions. Thought Machine aims to encourage openness and will support staff who raise genuine concerns in good faith under this policy, even if they turn out to be mistaken. Employees must not suffer any detrimental treatment as a result of raising a concern in good faith. Detrimental treatment includes dismissal, disciplinary action, threats or other unfavourable treatment connected with raising a concern. If an employee believes that they have suffered any such treatment, they should inform the Chief People Officer immediately.
