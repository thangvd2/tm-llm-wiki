# Programme Definition

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The purpose of programme definition is to articulate what the intent of the programme is so that all stakeholders have a clear understanding of what needs to be achieved and how success will be measured. This involves defining:

-   **Vision:** A concise statement, usually developed in conjunction with senior stakeholders and the sponsor, of the future state the programme will deliver. It is the guiding light setting the direction for everyone involved. Without this, there will not be a clear path to achieving it.
    
-   **Goals and objectives:** Details of what the programme is setting out to achieve on completion and is used to measure the success of failure of a programme.
    
-   **Scope:** Set the boundaries of what is to be delivered and in turn define exactly what goals, deadlines, and project deliverables the team will work towards. Clarifying scope, avoids misunderstandings, miscommunications, and the potential for scope creep that can lead to delivery overruns. Importantly, what is categorically out of scope also needs to be defined.
    
-   **Key success factors:** What needs to be accomplished for the programme to be deemed successful. These help to provide focus and assess whether the initiative is achieving its intended goals.
    
-   **Sponsorship:** identification of the overall programme sponsor and sponsor(s) for specific phases of the programme.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Programme definition should be developed in conjunction with the Delivery & Phasing Plan and the Business Case & Budget. In the initial stages of a programme, the programme definition is likely to undergo several iterations as different options are considered until the preferred option is refined.

In addition to the overarching programme definition, each phase of the programme should also be defined with details of how the phase aligns with the overall programme’s objectives and goals.

As the programme definition is a living artefact, it is important that it is reviewed, updated, and revised over time to reflect the current state of the programme. Any changes should be a conscious decision, so the impact is fully understood, communicated, and adopted.

### [](#vision "Copy link to heading")Vision

-   The vision should be developed in conjunction with the sponsors to provide a \`north star' for the programme. A link between the vision and leadership is critical for success as it provides a reference point for the programme and supports efficient and effective decision making.
    
-   The vision is a high level statement of intent; it will be challenging yet achievable and represent the programme’s long term goals.
    

*"Our new business plan aims to take the fintech challenge head-on. This is why we decided to create a new entirely-digital bank to serve our younger retail customers, who prefer not to use branches and don’t require the same in-person service that our exclusive, affluent and private multichannel clients do. This new digital bank will evolve our retail business from incumbent to fintech challenger in the mass market, with the option to expand internationally. We chose Thought Machine as our partner due to its international standing as a fintech innovator." Carlo Messina, Intesa Sanpaolo CEO*

### [](#goals_and_objectives "Copy link to heading")Goals and Objectives

-   Goals and objectives provide a tangible set of outcomes that can be objectively measured. The objectives in conjunction with each other represent the realisation of the vision. All objectives should be SMART. For example:
    
    -   To complete a migration of all accounts from current legacy systems onto Vault Core over the next 5 years.
        
    -   To enable the annual Cost to Income ratio to be reduced to <30% within 3 years.
        
    -   To launch a new savings product into the market on a fully cloud native technology stack in under 12 months.
        
    

### [](#scope "Copy link to heading")Scope

-   Scope should set clear boundaries for the programme to operate within. It is important that scope isn’t ambiguous and it can be valuable to define what is out of scope as well as what is in scope.
    

error

Watch out for scope creep or gold plating! When additional features are added to scope without formal agreement, this can cause costs to spiral and delivery dates to be missed.

### [](#key_success_factors "Copy link to heading")Key Success Factors

-   Key success factors define the critical elements or conditions that must be met for the programme to achieve its intended objectives and deliver its desired outcomes.
    
-   Success factors help align stakeholders and ensure the programme remains aligned with business goals, is well-executed, and can deliver value effectively.
    
-   Statements of success can also be written looking to the future. Irrespective of the approach taken, like the vision, this view of what success looks like should be transparent to all involved.
    

*"…Success would depend on us recognizing and responding to our clients' needs for simpler, faster, and better banking." Deniz Güven, previous Mox CEO*

### [](#sponsorship "Copy link to heading")Sponsorship

-   Establishing a sponsor for the overall programme is critical. The sponsor must be someone who:
    
    -   Acts as the primary advocate for the programme.
        
    -   Takes ultimate accountability for benefit realisation.
        
    -   Plays an active role in defining and shaping the direction of the programme.
        
    -   Secures the budget and necessary resources.
        
    -   Works across organisational and functional boundaries to influence and secure the buy-in from the necessary stakeholders.
        
    
-   It is vital to have sponsors from the business who are engaged in setting the vision and defining the business case; these sponsors may vary by phase. Business sponsorship ensures the programme delivers tangible business benefits and not just technical success.
    

error

Failure to obtain sponsorship from an appropriate executive level within the business can see programmes stall, struggle to obtain budget and ultimately fail.
