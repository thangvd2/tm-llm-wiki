# Customer Comms

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Depending on the type of production event, the impact on the end customer may vary significantly. Customer communication is important where there is material impact to the customers experience, perhaps new or deprecated services, where there may be an customer channel outage or degradation in service for a period, or where there are changes to the Terms & Conditions of their relationship with the client or the products they have.

Some such communications are important simply to manage customer expectations and to minimise the number of customer complaints/contacts around the period of the production event - “I am trying to access my account however by mobile applications seems to be offline”. Other communications will be mandatory given regulations the client is governed by e.g. changes to T&Cs or in the case of a migration, perhaps the customers relationship with a new banking entity.

It is critical to ensure that any regulated customer communication is followed as per requirements else there becomes a risk that the execution of the event itself could be delayed or that there could be subsequent action by the regulator.

It is also important to consider preparation of customer communications in the event of an unexpected occurrence during the production event that might for example lead to an unanticipated outage. In the majority of cases this preparatory work may ultimately not have been necessary, however if an issue were to occur, particularly where it wasn’t deemed necessary to notify customers in advance, then having proactively prepared a customer communication could save time, allow focus to be applied to recovery activity and minimise ‘noise’ from customers who are affected.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Customer communication will use the clients chosen channels as appropriate to their customer base. Channels might include physical letters, email, SMS, push notifications, website notifications or wider marketing activity.

Each form of communication is likely to require it’s own governance and set of approvals and sign offs before delivery. With reference to the pre-emptive creation of a customer communication for an unexpected customer outage, such approvals can introduce a lead time before the communication can ultimately be made. Getting such approval for content and distribution in advance has the potential to significantly reduce the negative affect on the relationship with the customer of the communication of disruption can be sent quickly and efficiently.

Even where it will not be possible to accurately predict the exact nature of the communication content, considering approvals for the channel or distribution list or simply having approvers on standby can still be an advantage.
