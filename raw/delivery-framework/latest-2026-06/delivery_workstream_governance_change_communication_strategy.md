# Change and Communication Strategy

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

It is important to recognise that a core banking transformation involves significant changes to process, technology and people; this can create uncertainty and resistance for both the stakeholders both directly and indirectly involved. A well-crafted Change and Communication Strategy is essential for guiding stakeholders through change with clarity and confidence.

By proactively managing how changes are communicated and engaging with stakeholders throughout the process, clients can significantly enhance the success of their core banking transformation.

A well defined Change & Communication Strategy facilitates:

-   **Smoother transition** to the target state by helping stakeholders understand the change, why it’s happening, and what it means for them.
    
-   **Buy-in to the changes** through transparent, timely, and targeted communication building stakeholder trust and confidence.
    
-   **Faster adoption** proactive planning, combined with clear communication and support mechanisms, speeds up adoption of the changes and minimises disruption.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

-   A Change and Communication Strategy provides a clear framework for planning, executing, and evaluating change initiatives, ensuring that all stakeholders are effectively engaged and informed throughout the process. A well-defined methodology helps clients to navigate the complexities of change, minimising resistance, and maximising the likelihood of a successful outcome.
    
-   Inputs into developing the Change and Communication Strategy:
    
    -   **Vision and objectives:** Articulate the key objectives of the transformation, including what success looks like and how it will be measured; this is defined further in [Programme Definition](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance/programme_definition).
        
    -   **Initial change impact analysis:** Determine the impact of the change on various parts of the client organisation, including processes, roles, systems, and culture. Identify key areas that will require support.
        
    -   **Readiness assessment:** Identify potential barriers and enablers to successful implementation through evaluating readiness for change by assessing stakeholder sentiment, past experiences with change, and the current organisational climate.
        
    -   **Stakeholder analysis:** Identify all stakeholders affected by the change. Assess their influence, interest, support levels, and communication needs. For each stakeholder group consider:
        
        -   Can you prioritise stakeholders based on the amount of power and influence they have on the programme?
            
        -   Are their existing relationships in place? If so, what?
            
        -   What are the current views of the programme?
            
        -   What do you know about their preferred engagement channels? For example, customer online activity and social channels.
            
        -   Who influences their opinions? And are their influencers also key stakeholders?
            
        
    
-   Develop the Change and Communication Strategy to include:
    
    -   **Messages:** Craft clear and consistent messages that convey the purpose, benefits, and impacts of the change. Tailor messages for different stakeholders to ensure relevance and details of what is expected and action required.
        
    -   **Communication channels:** Identify the most effective channels to reach stakeholders, such as emails, town halls, intranet updates, or team meetings. Use a mix of channels to reinforce messages.
        
    -   **Engagement plan:** Design a plan to actively engage stakeholders throughout the change process. This includes identifying touchpoints for feedback, involvement opportunities, and regular updates including details on what information will be shared and when.
        
    -   **Training and support needs:** Determine what training, resources, and support are needed to help stakeholders adapt to the change. Develop a plan to deliver these resources effectively.
        
    -   **Monitoring and Fixing:** Set-out the approach to monitoring the adoption of changes through feedback, performance metrics, and stakeholder input and how to adopt changes quickly based in response to feedback.
        
    -   **Embedding Changes:** define the approach to making the new ways of working part of the client organisation’s processes and culture. This would include continuous reinforcement from leadership, regular communication, and alignment with business goals.
        
    

### [](#key_stakeholders "Copy link to heading")Key Stakeholders

Ensure that all stakeholders impacted by a change are identified and the impact is fully understood; the breadth, approach and level of impact will vary by phase.

Key stakeholders considerations:

 
| Stakeholder | Considerations |
| --- | --- |
| 
Programme Team Members

 | 

These people will drive the transformation and are key ambassadors. Team members who are fully engaged in their work are more likely to be motivated, produce high-quality work and spread positive messages across the client organisation and to customers.

 |
| 

Wider Organisation Stakeholders

 | 

There will be dependencies on functions outside of the programme for the programme to be successful for example input from SMEs, security approvals, downstream reporting changes. The earlier these stakeholders are engaged and understand their role in supporting the programme, the easier it will be to gain their support and avoid delays.

 |
| 

Employees

 | 

Change brings opportunities for career development with new roles, potential for promotion and investment in training. On the other hand there may be resentment if not selected for the desired role, concern about job security and the ability to adapt to changes in ways of working. Organisation need to ensure the retention of top talent and knowledge retention of legacy systems will be key to support migration activities.

 |
| 

Customers

 | 

Existing and new customers can reap the benefits of new products, features and services and this increases competition in the market. Customers with accounts that are being migrated may be impacted by changes to existing terms and conditions, transitioning to new channels or removals of services; there may be a legal requirement to inform them of these changes. Proactive communication should be focused on minimising the amount of unnecessary customer queries or data changes during the change window allowing the delivery team to focus on a successful go-live event.

 |
| 

Community

 | 

Impact on the local economy can affect an organisation’s brand. On the positive side, it may lead to the creation of new job opportunities in the area and expansion of services that support local business. On the other hand, it could bring potential job losses, closure of bank branches reducing the number of people and spending in the area and a loss of customer loyalty.

 |
| 

Shareholders

 | 

There could be high exceptions to see a growth in profits and a faster return on investment. Transformation can increase shareholder value, but it can also significantly harm it if not managed well.

 |
| 

Regulator

 | 

Regulators play a key role in any transformation; there may be a dependency on a banking licence, approvals required for changes to products or the introduction of new products or services or nervousness about the introduction of cloud technology as it is not something they are familiar with.Regulatory requirements vary by region, however it is recommended that regulators are engaged early and kept abreast of developments as it could significantly delay or worse stop delivery.

 |
