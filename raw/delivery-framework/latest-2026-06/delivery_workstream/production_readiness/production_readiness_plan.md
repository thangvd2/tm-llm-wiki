# Production Readiness Plan

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The Production Readiness plan should not be seen as a testing plan for a given change. Planning and execution of functional, non-functional, integration, end-to-end and user acceptance testing etc. is the domain of the areas of the programme accountable for the end product.

The purpose of the Production Readiness Plan is to ensure that there is appropriate coordination of the affected stakeholders in the value chain throughout the life-cycle of the change event, that all of the pre-requisite activities required to be completed and the outputs expected ahead of any event execution are known and the timelines are transparent. It must also deal with the ‘what-if’ unhappy path scenarios that could occur and provide clear guidance on what must happen as a result.

The intention should be to have a comprehensive plan of activities that, when executed, enables the subject of the change event to be smoothly transitioned and adopted into production BAU state.

Ultimately the plan exists to minimise the overall risk relating to the change event itself and the subsequent adoption into BAU. Given that potential issues will have been predicted and planned for, response to issues during the event will be able to be dealt with in a calm, controlled and methodical manner.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

The plan should include, but not be limited to the following information:

-   Timing and scheduling
    
    -   A clear timeline of activities including estimated duration of activities.
        
    -   Any critical cut off times for specific activities or the event itself.
        
    
-   Stakeholder RACI
    
    -   Defines who is responsible for individual activities; who needs to be consulted or informed before, during and after the execution of a specific activity.
        
    -   Critically, who is accountable for making any go/no-go/rollback type decisions before, during of after the event.
        
    
-   Dependencies and Assumptions
    
    -   Critical path dependencies and pre-requisite requirements.
        
    -   Assumptions for which there is consequence or decisioning to be made depending on the true/false nature of the assumption.
        
    
-   Happy Path / Unhappy Path / Failure Scenario activities
    
    -   Sets of activities specific to the occurrence of a predicted issue or types of issues.
        
    -   May include rollback activities only executed in a worst case scenario.
        
    
-   KPIs
    
    -   Incremental indicators that can be used to measure progress against the plan.
        
    -   May be used to as inputs during any event checkpoints to aid decisioning (continue/pause/rollback).
        
    

However small, simple or standard the change event, there is still value in having a Production Readiness Plan, though the depth and detail of the plan may vary from that of a large, complex and irregular event. Occasionally, issues do occur either as a result of unexpected or extraordinary events or even complacency. It is better to be prepared for such events, even if this is simply to have a predefined escalation process, than for the team to find themselves reacting under pressure unsure what steps to take or what the impact the wrong step might have. This preparation will likely result in reduced potential downtime, reduced customer impact and lower cost of any remediating steps.
