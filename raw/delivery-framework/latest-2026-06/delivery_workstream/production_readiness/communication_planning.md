# Communication Planning

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Whereas the wider programme will have a formal communications plan, the purpose of this activity is to define a communications plan specific to an individual change event.

The purpose of the communications plan is to ensure that each affected stakeholder is communicated the correct and necessary information, at the appropriate time (that may be more than once) via a specified and sanctioned medium.

The plan must also define the cadence, scope and stakeholder attendees of any event focused governance forums and it must define appropriate feedback mechanisms, both ad-hoc and formal via reports etc. These forums themselves will contribute as an input to the go/no go decisioning being taken prior to execution.

Importantly, the Communications Plan is there to ensure that any mandatory compliance and regulatory reporting and/or communication is adhered to.

Ultimately, the plan’s purpose is to ensure

-   The coordination of activities and dependencies across stakeholder groups.
    
-   To provide continuous transparency to affected stakeholders in relation to the status or planning and execution of activities.
    
-   To manage and minimise risk relating to the change event and to ensure that stakeholders are well-informed, engaged, and supportive of the change event.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

All stakeholders in the value chain will require some level of communication before, during or after the event. It is important to plan in advance to whom communications need to be sent to, the medium that will be used and the message that needs to be sent. Without a pre-meditated and clear plan, there is an increased risk that a key stakeholder will not be informed of something important or that key personnel will not be able to be contacted in a pressure situation.

-   Identify each stakeholder and their individual information needs.
    
-   Tailor the communication approach to engage and inform stakeholders across the value chain from customer, to branch/customer support staff, channels teams, finance, compliance, risk, shared services (e.g. Databases and networks), execs and regulators depending on the nature of the change and the impact assessment.
    
-   Prepare as many of the communications as possible in advance.
    

### [](#pre_event "Copy link to heading")Pre-event:

-   Market the change event and notify affected stakeholders.
    
    -   Convey the timelines for and the purpose, benefits and impact of the change event. Tailor to different stakeholders.
        
    
-   Service disruption notices.
    
    -   Provide notice of any periods of disruption to BAU services. This may be direct to customers or to support staff in anticipation of customer queries.
        
    
-   Testing status forums.
    
    -   To provide incremental updates on the progress of any tests upon which a go decision might be dependent.
        
    
-   Dependency tracking.
    
    -   Provide a forum where any activities on the critical path can be monitored and the knock-on impact of any delay can be measured.
        
    
-   Stand-by resource readiness.
    
    -   Provide notice to those responsible for happy-path activities, to ensure those who might be required in the event of an unexpected (but planned for) incident are prepared.
        
    
-   Heightened Awareness
    
    -   For some parties, it may be advisable to enact a state of heightened awareness in advance of the event. While the individual, team or third party may not have to do anything out of the ordinary, with warning they may be able to monitor for specific communications more diligently or prepare to provide responses in a more timely manner than may be the norm.
        
    

### [](#event_execution "Copy link to heading")Event execution:

-   Production Readiness Plan.
    
    -   The plan itself may include tasks to send specific communications during the event e.g. to provide notification of the completion of an activity. The Communication Plan may contain the granular detail of who to contact and how is specific scenarios that play out during execution of the event.
        
    
-   Broadcast status updates.
    
    -   Status Broadcasts - Where there are a large number of stakeholders who need to be informed of high level progress or specific events, prepare Distribution Lists (or other channels) in advance with the required audience pre-populated. This will minimise the chances that key individuals or groups are missed from a communication in the heat of the event.
        
    -   Exec. level updates - Lower level updates may be required that are tailored to programme sponsors and other executives. It is advisable to have a Distribution List and to have drafted or at least templated communications in advance to ensure that updates include all salient points.
        
    
-   Escalations.
    
    -   At times it will be necessary to raise an escalation in response to an issue, an unexpected outcome or delay; usually where additional, unplanned support is required or a decision needs to be taken. Where as a status broadcast might be quite a passive communication that recipients may or may not read with urgency, an escalation needs to reach it’s intended recipient promptly and be acted upon. The method of escalation needs to be planned in advance and the escalation path agreed. There is little point escalating to someone via a workplace messaging application if that person is offline and only available via a personal phone number. Likewise, escalating to the wrong person, someone who is not empowered or skilled to provide the right information or decision may waste valuable time.
        
    -   If the escalation path involves 3rd parties, it is critical that communication channels are documented and appropriate process is followed. It is possible the escalation to these groups may require people with minimal context of the event to support. It is critical that these escalations be made with all of the necessary information for the 3rd party to be able to take prompt and appropriate action. Pre-event communication to enact Heightened Awareness can help in such scenarios.
        
    
-   Customer services.
    
    -   During the event, depending on how well executed and received customer communications were, Customer Services will be on the front line when end users encounter unexpected issues e.g. that they can’t access and account or can’t see a live balance. It is critical to keep Customer Service teams informed in order that the can provide a clear and unambiguous response to customer queries. Critically, of end customer impact is expected, Customer Services need to be kept informed of when the impact will start, the duration of impact and when the event is completed and business as usual has returned.
        
    -   Given it is likely unexpected issues post-event will be reported to the Customer Services team, they should also be informed of how to escalate and to whom, any reported issues so that they can be investigated.
        
    -   It is also important to keep Customer Services and other Operations users informed of progress where their own day-to-day activities might be affected. If the event involves a migration of a customer account, a new feature of a product of the release of a new operations channel, the user will need be aware of what accounts might be serviced in the legacy system versus the target core, how and when they can activate the new product feature for customers or when they need to start using the new channel.
        
    

### [](#post_event "Copy link to heading")Post-event:

-   Formal Acceptance
    
    -   After the event formal acceptance of the change might need to be requested and provided. Such acceptance might be the trigger for the release team to stand down or for the change to be formally handed over into BAU. If acceptance isn’t provided, it may be that a rollback needs to be performed or remediation activity performed.
        
    
-   Support Comms and Handover
    
    -   After the event it is important to ensure there is a plan to formally communicate handover to the team(s) responsible for ongoing support. The communication might include documents (or where to find documents) such as a ‘What’s new guide?', detailed release notes and support guidance. While these documents could be shared in advance, formal communication after the release will confirm the handover of responsibility.
        
    -   The team(s) responsible for the change might also include guidance around any specific activity to monitor for for a period of time post release, contact details should anything unexpected be observed, and details of any warranty period that might be applicable.
        
    
-   Closure/completion comms.
    
    -   If communications have been sent during the event to any affected groups, e.g. customers, executives or other parties involved in the release, it is likely further communications will need to be send to the same groups once the event has been completed, reusing any distribution lists created to ensure continuity of communication.
        
    -   The nature of the communication might be different for each group; for customers an overview of the changes and how these affect them in a positive way, for internal groups the communication might be simply a thank-you, a summary of the objective now realised and/or a notification that all those actively involved or on stand-by can stand down.
        
    -   It is important to avoid situations where people are left in limbo, unaware if the event has completed or not.
        
    
-   Retrospectives.
    
    -   Following any event, it’s often recommended to hold a retrospective to discuss lessons learned and positives that should be reenforced. After the retrospective, it’s a good idea to communicate the output including any actions that are being taken as a result.
