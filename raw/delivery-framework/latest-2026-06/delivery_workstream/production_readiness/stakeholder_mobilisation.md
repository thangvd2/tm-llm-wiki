# Stakeholder Mobilisation

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The act of Stakeholder mobilisation is intended to ensure that each stakeholder involved in the execution of the change event and the immediate aftermath is aware of the dependencies on them in terms of actions, timelines and capacity.

Those stakeholders need to commit to the availability required and any risk of unavailability or reduced available should be managed.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

### [](#variable_lead_times "Copy link to heading")Variable lead times

Different stakeholder groups, in particular those of shared service teams, vendors or other third parties perhaps not directly involved in the change event, may have longer lead times to mobilise or even place on stand-by, resources required during the event. Consider the critical path for the event and aim to address early any dependencies with long lead time for mobilisation.

### [](#geographical_availability "Copy link to heading")Geographical availability

If geographically dispersed individuals or teams need to be ‘on site’ then this will need to be managed. Likewise, where individuals or teams operate out of different geographies and timelines this can affect availability and the ability to mobilise resources at the necessary time.

### [](#availability_windows "Copy link to heading")Availability windows

Some stakeholders may only be able to mobilise resources for a specific window of time. If these resources are critical this needs to be taken into account in the overall plan of execution. If for example the window is closing, is there a known point in the plan beyond which the point of no return is crossed where a stop/continue checkpoint is needed?

### [](#decision_forums_and_war_rooms "Copy link to heading")Decision Forums and War-rooms

On occasion it may be necessary to convene an emergency war-room during an event or to mobilise a sponsor/executive to make a critical decision. Even where these events are held only in extraordinary circumstances, the ability to contact such decision makers and how to provide them with sufficient context needs to be taken into consideration. If an in-person or virtual war-room is required, are the physical or online facilities prepared and joining information available?
