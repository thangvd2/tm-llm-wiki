# Regulator Comms

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Put simply, the purpose of Regulator Communications is to ensure that the regulator(s) are kept abreast of production events that could have a regulatory impact. What qualifies will differ per event and per regulator however, notification of an event will give the regulator an opportunity to inform the client of any communications or suchlike that it feels are necessary to be made to the clients customers or other stakeholders. This may include expected timelines for communications, channels and even whether the client can take unilateral action assuming ‘opt-in’ by the customer or whether it must get explicit ‘opt-in’ by the impacted customer.

Such examples might include:

-   Result in changes in T&Cs for a product or customer.
    
-   Change the customers relationship with the client e.g. the migration of a customer account from one entity to another or the migration of an account resulting in change of account details etc.
    

Failure to engage the regulator \[at appropriate times or with necessary information\] could lead to any number of outcomes, many of which could affect the success of the production event or future production events.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

### [](#regulatory_compliance "Copy link to heading")Regulatory Compliance

-   Financial regulators (like the FDIC, OCC, FCA, etc.) require that financial institutions maintain compliance with laws and regulations throughout any operational change.
    
-   System upgrades can impact areas such as data integrity, customer privacy, reporting capabilities, and transaction monitoring, all of which are heavily regulated.
    
-   Early communication ensures the upgrade won’t inadvertently lead to non-compliance.
    

### [](#operational_risk_management "Copy link to heading")Operational Risk Management

-   Regulators want to ensure that a financial institution has a sound plan for managing operational risks during any upgrade or other production event.
    
-   This includes business continuity planning, disaster recovery, and cybersecurity controls.
    
-   Keeping the regulator informed shows the bank is proactively managing risks.
    

### [](#customer_impact "Copy link to heading")Customer Impact

-   If the production event affects customer-facing systems (e.g. online banking, payments, etc.), the regulator will want to know how disruptions will be minimised and communicated to customers.
    
-   It’s especially important if there’s any planned downtime or changes to how customer data is handled.
    

### [](#regulatory_reporting_systems "Copy link to heading")Regulatory Reporting Systems

-   Production events might affect how reports are generated and submitted (e.g., suspicious activity reports, liquidity coverage ratios).
    
-   Regulators must ensure that the financial institution can still meet timely and accurate reporting obligations during and after the production event.
    

### [](#supervisory_relationship_and_transparency "Copy link to heading")Supervisory Relationship and Transparency

-   Maintaining open communication strengthens the trust and transparency between the financial institution and its regulator(s).
    
-   Proactive notification can prevent misunderstandings or regulatory concerns later.
    

### [](#post_implementation_review "Copy link to heading")Post-Implementation Review

-   Regulators may require a post-implementation review or audit to verify that the system change meets standards.
    
-   Early engagement helps align expectations and requirements for such reviews.
