# Business Capability Evaluation

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

A business capability evaluation focuses on identifying the capabilities (e.g. customer onboarding, retail deposit account servicing, reporting, fraud, account system of record, etc) and impact to those capabilities based upon the financial institution’s modernization plan. This is needed so that the financial institution can start identifying what solutions, legacy or new, in the ISV ecosystem are needed in addition to Vault Core.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Understanding the entire bank system landscape is vital to ensuring all necessary business capabilities are covered in the target state. This includes onboarding and servicing, branch and call centre, data, reporting, enterprise general ledger, core functions among others. In general the capabilities can be classified in three categories:

1.  Capabilities provided by Vault (fully or partially).
    
2.  Capabilities not provided by Vault but where the legacy core currently supports (gaps).
    
3.  Capabilities currently provided outside the legacy core.
    

The primary focus of this evaluation is on the third area (capabilities currently provided outside the legacy core). In order to complete the evaluation effectively the financial institution should:

1.  Create a conceptual architecture / bank capability view.
    
2.  Map their current capabilities to it, and overlay existing systems (skip if greenfield).  
    
3.  Utilise the minimal viable proposition definition to identify and prioritise the capabilities that will be impacted first (colour coded via a heatmap).  
    
4.  Identify capabilities that are gaps or where it is known that the legacy system will be replaced. For these identified gaps or known system replacements, understand ISV options available in the market. This can be done using [ISV Ecosystem Market Scan](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/business/isv_ecosystem).
    
5.  As more legacy back-book or new products are added via the product roadmap, subsequent capabilities are identified and the capability view is iterated upon. In this way, business capability views build off each other as more products (e.g. retail deposits first followed by retail mortgages) or functions are added (e.g. digital first and then branch added later).
