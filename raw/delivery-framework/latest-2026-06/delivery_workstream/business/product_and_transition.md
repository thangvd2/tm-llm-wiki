# Product and Transition Roadmap

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The purpose of this activity is to build upon the output of the Product Strategy and MVP Proposition Definition by sequencing all product launches onto a timeline.

This timeline acts a bridge between the bank’s current state and its desired target state with regard to products, showing how the current legacy products are added to, altered, cut down, and rationalised over the course of the programme as it moves through its key transition states.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

### [](#when_to_create "Copy link to heading")When to create

-   The Product & Transition Roadmap is normally created early on in the modernisation programme lifecycle, often as an input to the programme’s Business Case.  
    
-   It will be iterated upon throughout the programme lifecycle.
    
    -   The first version can be considered a statement of intent that is built upon a set of high-level assumptions, which is then updated as the programme matures and assumptions are either crystallised or deviated from during delivery.
        
    

### [](#level_to_create_at "Copy link to heading")Level to create at

-   For relatively simple programmes that are launching or migrating just a few product types, the Product & Transition Roadmap should cover the entire programme scope.
    
    -   E.g. programme scope is launching a new BNPL offering and then migrating an existing Current Account and Fixed Term Deposit book.
        
    
-   For more complex programmes that span many years, geographies, brands, business lines, etc. then it’s likely that over the course of the overall programme multiple Product & Transition Roadmaps will need to be created at a variety of levels.
    
    -   E.g. programme scope is migrating out a dozen core banking systems spread across six geographies representing a full retail and corporate banking product offering.
        
    -   In practice, a roadmap will be required at the geography / brand level first, with a subsequent deeper focus on each geography / brand with the aim to execute the detailed product roadmap as outlined in this guidance.
        
    
-   Working to detailed focus
    
    -   Start at the macro programme scope and articulate this at a high level, then focus and dive deeper as more information is uncovered. E.g. total programme is migration of Current Account  
        Savings Account + Loans → deep dive to Phase 2 delivery of just the Current Account migration → deep dive to isolate the Current Account product variants in scope → deep dive to isolate the account statuses in scope for migration and thus migration volumes. This makes it much easier to navigate programme content and helps embed consistency to the programme objectives and scope.
        
    

### [](#format_tooling_to_use "Copy link to heading")Format / Tooling to use

-   Anything from a simple set of powerpoint slides to a much more detailed draw.io / Lucidchart diagram can work well, with this decision largely driven by the complexity of the programme (how many products are being sequenced, what else is the programme doing other than core replacement, etc.).
    

### [](#information_to_include "Copy link to heading")Information to include

The Product & Transition Roadmap should capture the go-live plan for…

-   *…all products including both greenfield and legacy products, where applicable…*
    
    -   Greenfield = new products launched on target
        
    -   Legacy = products currently on / off sale on legacy.
        
        See [Product Rationalisation](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/business/product_rationalisation) for more details on deciding between greenfield and legacy products.
        
    
-   *…covering both Front-Book launches/switches and Back-Book migrations…*
    
    -   Each product needs to answer the questions:
        
        -   What needs to happen to the front-book for this product?
            
        -   What needs to happen to the back-book (legacy only) for this product?
            
        
    -   For legacy products in particular there is a matrix of options when answering the above, depending on whether the front-book or back-book on target will be an equivalent product (faithfully recreated) or a different product resulting from product rationalisation.
        
    -   For example, a legacy product may have its front-book switched to a new innovative product with new T&Cs (effectively taking the legacy version of the product off sale) but the back-book is nevertheless migrated to an equivalent version of the legacy product recreated on target and then run down over time. Or perhaps the back-book is run-off on source, or forcefully converted to a product with different T&Cs?  
        
    
-   *…sequenced on a timeline…*
    
    -   The short-term programme priorities should have already been established through the Product Strategy and MVP Proposition Definition activities.
        
        -   In keeping with the \`think big, start small' mantra, most programmes begin with a simple product and scale up over time to the more complex (often credit or corporate) use cases.
            
        -   Sequence the go-lives accordingly - including dates where possible, but if not then simply in order in the abstract.
            
        
    -   The timeline is key in visualising the product strategy and its aggregate impact on the customer offering / experience over the course of the programme (e.g. over which period are customers split across digital apps, unable to be efficiently serviced by ops colleagues who need to look at two sets of ops screens, limited in the products they can open, etc.).
        
    -   Other factors that influence the timeline sequencing could include:
        
        -   Known programme start / end dates or fixed go-live timings (perhaps driven by regulatory or market commitments)
            
        -   Design decisions or principles about the relative priority of particular brands, business lines, products, etc.
            
        
    
-   *…considering / overlaying other relevant product information (such as)…*
    
    -   High-level product rationalisation for legacy products
        
        -   Optional conversion,
            
        -   Forced conversion, etc.
            
        
    -   High-level go-live tranching
        
        -   Colleague pilots,
            
        -   Limited customer betas,
            
        -   Phased migrations, etc.
            
        
    -   The treatment strategy for any legacy product that is not in scope for migration
        
        -   Run-off,
            
        -   Demise, etc.
            
        
    -   Phases of customer incentivisation
        
        -   Switch offers,
            
        -   Preferable rates,
            
        -   Taking products off sale, etc.
            
        
    
-   *…overlaying the programme’s other non-product transition states and showing how the product go-lives interlock with these…*
    
    -   Though certainly not a guarantee at this early stage of the programme lifecycle, where any decisions or assumptions have already been made for any of the other programme workstreams these should be inputs to the Product & Transition Roadmap process, as they may have a bearing on product go-live planning.
        
    -   See the Gather non-product inputs section below for more details and example inputs, but in particular the following should be considered:
        
        -   Digital app strategy (see the [Governance Workstream](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance)).
            
        -   Architectural co-existence strategy (see the [Architecture Workstream](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/architecture)).
            
        -   Non-core banking system migrations (see the [Integrations Workstream](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/integrations)).
            
        -   Colleague servicing / operations strategy (see the [Production Readiness Workstream](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/production_readiness)).
            
        
    
-   *…and is normally accompanied by written justifications for the decisions taken.*
    
    -   Alongside the roadmap diagram there should be some written justification of the decisions taken, which will be an important input into the programme’s Business Case.

### [](#additional_guidance "Copy link to heading")Additional Guidance

#### [](#what_is_a_transition_state "Copy link to heading")What is a Transition State?

Core banking modernisation programme delivery is normally broken down at the highest level into \`transition states'. A transition state is a phase of programme delivery that culminates in a major delivery milestone that delivers a tangible business or technology benefit that moves the programme closer to achieving its ultimate target state.

Transition states should: - Adhere to the overarching delivery principle of think big, start small. - Inform programme org structure / delivery workstream breakdown, but not dictate it. - Execute in parallel where appropriate. - Map to one of the programme’s value drivers.

The Product & Transition Roadmap plots a programme’s product related transition states onto a high-level timeline (or \`Spine of the Plan') that can inform lower-level delivery planning.

Though it is first and foremost a product-centric deliverable, it is good practice to also overlay key transition states relating to the programme’s other workstreams. For example, launch of a new digital app, switch of colleague operational screens, entering or exiting a period of architectural co-existence, etc. These non-product transition states can materially change the approach taken to the product go-lives by materially altering the customer experience, programme delivery feasibility, underlying risk, etc.

There is no one size fits all approach, and a programme’s transition states and their ordering is heavily dependant on specific value drives and scope.

**Example transition states in broadly chronological order could include:**

 
| Transition State | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 
Legal Day 1

 | 

Change in legal ownership of a bank / brand - mostly relevant for migrations resulting from divestment or acquisition.

 |
| 

Architectural Coexistence Enabled

 | 

Implementing the architectural \`scaffolding' that allows for multiple core banking systems / tech stacks to exist side-by-side. Messages are routed and consolidated as required by either upstream or downstream stakeholders.

 |
| 

Operating Model Cutover

 | 

Switching an element of business operations from legacy to target, for example switching the telephony operations from legacy to target systems / people / processes. Where there are multiple discrete operating model changes occurring then this transition state can be broken down accordingly.

 |
| 

Digital App Launch

 | 

Launching a new digital app and switching customers from the legacy version.

 |
| 

Greenfield Product Launch

 | 

Launching a product unrelated to the legacy products / accounts (and is therefore not intended to be a migration target).

 |
| 

Non Core Banking System Migrations

 | 

Executing migrations into systems other than Vault Core (where these migrations decouple from the Vault Core load). For example, migration of customer data into a target Customer Core months ahead of a core banking system migration.

 |
| 

Harmonisation / Simplification

 | 

Making legacy look like target in advance of a front-book switch or back-book migration. For example, rationalising products, changing T&Cs, altering processes, etc.

 |
| 

Front-Book Product Switch(es) / Launch(es)

 | 

Move the front-book (new account onboarding) of a legacy product to the target platform, or launch a new greenfield offering. Moving the front-book in advance of migration provides a period of product proving at a relatively low volume.

 |
| 

Back-Book Product Migration(s)

 | 

Move the back-book of existing accounts to the target platform. This transition state represents the execution of the end-to-end [migration delivery lifecycle](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/migration#activities).

 |
| 

Decommissioning

 | 

Archive, warehouse, delete, and remove legacy systems / data that is no longer required in the target tech stack.

 |

#### [](#inputs_to_the_product_transition_roadmap "Copy link to heading")Inputs to the Product & Transition Roadmap

Before the development of the Product & Transition Roadmap, it is recommended to undertake the following prerequisite steps:

**Prioritise the programme’s value drivers**

-   Confirm the business benefits that the programme is intending to achieve and prioritise them, as per the [Governance Workstream](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance).
    
-   Knowing which specific drivers are most important to the programme is going to be relevant when determining programme transition states, product prioritisation/sequencing and lower level delivery strategies, as different approaches/decisions can either address or run counter to particular value drivers.
    

**Example value drivers**

The Product & Transition Roadmap (and all strategies that result from this, e.g. migration strategy) must align to the programme’s \`value drivers'.

Value drivers are the compelling benefit(s) to the business that justify the execution of the programme and are generally agreed during the initial business case and funding requests prior to programme delivery commencing.

Common value drivers for core banking modernisation programmes include:

| Common Business Value Drivers |
| --- |
| 
Common Business Value Drivers.

 |
| 

Reducing operating costs.

 |
| 

Launching new, innovative product propositions.

 |
| 

Streamlining the product proposition.

 |
| 

Streamlining business operations, such as removing manual processes.

 |
| 

Licensing deals and renewals with current core banking system provider(s).

 |
| 

Solving a key business risk or regulatory requirement.

 |
| 

Onshoring operations.

 |
| 

Acquisition of another bank’s back-book.

 |

| Common Technology Value Drivers |
| --- |
| 
Burning platform that is unable to take advantage of modern banking architecture: - Does not operate 24/7 (closes for batch processing) - Not cloud native - Written in code that has limited support - Monolithic architecture (inflexibility), etc.

 |
| 

Current technology has reached its End of Life.

 |

At the outset of the programme time must be invested to: (i) understand the value drivers and (ii) agree the relative priority of these value drivers, including the primary value driver which should ideally then reflect in the programme’s \`north star'.

**Define glossary of terms**

-   Define and agree a glossary of programme-related terms, to ensure all programme resources and documents use them consistently (including within the Product & Transition Roadmap itself).
    

**Example Glossary of Terms**

It is worryingly easy to talk at cross purposes when considering an isolated workstream delivery (e.g. migration workstream) rather than consider the programme as a whole. For example:

-   Calling a programme the \`X Product / Brand Migration Programme' is inherently confusing when the programme’s migration workstream is only going to execute a subset of the overall delivery scope (which also includes product build, target tech stack implementation, and other activities). Whenever anyone uses the term \`migration' are they referring to the programme as a whole or the slice of data migration work within the migration workstream specifically?
    
-   Is the \`new target Current Account' the innovative new product proposition that the Business are excited by, or is it the legacy product that exists today that is being (for the most part) recreated on target to facilitate a back-book migration?
    
-   Is \`dual run' the architectural state of running two core banking systems in parallel with a split book, or is it the migration strategy of decoupling migration load and cutover and running target in a shadow state for a period of time?
    

To cut through this ambiguity it is important to take the time to define a clear and concise glossary of key terms and then embed this within the programme from the very beginning.

These terms and their definitions can vary considerably from programme to programme. The following is a representative example of terms for core banking programmes:

**General**

 
| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 
Legacy

 | 

Pertaining to the current state, which the programme is seeking to move away from.

 |
| 

Target

 | 

Pertaining to the future state, which the programme is seeking to move to.

 |
| 

BAU

 | 

Business-as-usual, pertaining to the normal day-to-day running of the bank. For example, in a Vault Core context \`BAU API behaviour' is the behaviour of APIs used in normal operations, as opposed to the migration specific APIs outlined in \`using vault core’s migration apis'.

 |

**IT Architecture**

 
| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 
Legacy Tech Stack

 | 

The systems and services underpinning the current core banking solution.

 |
| 

Target Tech Stack

 | 

The systems and services underpinning the future core banking solution.

 |
| 

Customer Core

 | 

The data master for customer data, such as names, addresses, contact details, etc.

 |
| 

Core Banking System

 | 

The data master for account data, such as interest rates, end of day behaviour, financial movements, etc. Vault Core is a core banking system provided by Thought Machine.

 |
| 

Co-Existence

 | 

The architectural state of running two or more core banking systems side-by-side, which demands upstream routing and downstream consolidation of data between the cores.

 |
| 

Vault Core Integrations

 | 

The services that sit around Vault Core, orchestrating API messages. For example, an integration that sends account opening API requests to Vault Core.

 |

**Product**

 
| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 
Front-Book

 | 

Products that are currently offered to customers to open new accounts. Moving the onboarding of new accounts to target is a \`front-book switch'.

 |
| 

Back-Book

 | 

Existing accounts opened by customers at any point in the past. Moving the data of existing accounts to target is a \`back-book migration'.

 |
| 

Legacy Product

 | 

The product terms and conditions / behaviours as they manifest in the legacy tech stack.

 |
| 

Greenfield Product

 | 

The creation of a brand new product on target that bears no connection to the existing legacy products, and is thus not a target for an account migration.

 |
| 

Product Rationalisation

 | 

Consolidating legacy products, for example closing or moving old accounts onto a standardised set of terms and conditions. This reduces the number of product variants that need to be recreated on target to enable a data migration.

 |

**Business**

 
| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 
Operating Model

 | 

The blueprint for how resources should be organised and managed to achieve the bank’s overall vision / strategy.

 |
| 

\`\`X'' Operating Model Switch

 | 

A change between legacy and target that specifically does not involve the migration of any data. For example, switching customers from using the legacy version of a digital app to a new target version or changing front-end screens for operational colleagues in branch. \`Switch' is used here so as not to confuse with \`migration', which must always involve the movement of data. \`\`X'' here can be replaced with any system or capability to describe a switch that relates to that system or capability.

 |
| 

Account Switch

 | 

Customers electing to change from one product to another, eg. as the result of a marketing or incentive campaign.

 |

**Migration**

 
| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 
Migration

 | 

The movement of data from the legacy tech stack to the target tech stack. Comprises the sequential activities of load and cutover. Must not use \`migration' to refer to activities without data moving from point A to point B - instead see other more appropriate terms in this glossary.

 |
| 

Load

 | 

The data migration activity whereby data is loaded into the target core, in advance of cutover.

 |
| 

Cutover

 | 

The data migration activity whereby the target core banking system is made operationally live, following load. At cutover the system becomes the data master for a given account and the source of truth for other systems in the wider bank.

 |
| 

Harmonisation

 | 

Aligning the behaviour of legacy and target products / processes in advance of a migration, so that at the point of migration there is less change taking place. For example, halting processes that are fated, or changing product T&Cs.

 |
| 

\`\`X'' Migration

 | 

\`\`X'' here can be replaced with any system or capability to describe the migration of data relating to that system or capability. For example, \`Data warehouse migration' or \`Payments migration'. Useful distinction for programmes with multiple migration targets occurring at different transition states. In this context the migration of data into Vault Core can be referred to as the \`Account migration'.

 |

**Migration Strategies**

 
| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 
Big Bang

 | 

The migration strategy of migrating all accounts (and associated data) in scope of the programme in a single event.

 |
| 

Phased

 | 

The migration strategy of splitting the total scope into smaller chunks and migrating these incrementally.

 |
| 

Parallel Run

 | 

The migration strategy of decoupling migration load and cutover and running target in a shadow state for a period of time.

 |
| 

Onboard / Offboard

 | 

The migration strategy of closing accounts on the legacy core banking system and opening new accounts on the target core banking system (this is not strictly speaking a data migration).

 |
| 

Run-Off

 | 

The migration strategy of letting accounts end their life naturally on the legacy core banking system.

 |

**Gather product inputs**

-   Work with the Business function to gather basic information about the legacy product estate:
    
    -   Product categories and sub-categories (i.e. at a variety of levels, to give options when deciding level to create the roadmap at).
        
    -   Any decisions taken regarding product categories and sub-categories being in/out of programme scope.
        
    -   Account Volumes + Customer to Account mapping.
        
    

**Gather non-product inputs**

-   Understand the programme scope as currently defined to-date. This does not need to be a grand analytical exercise, but rather just surfacing the core scope intentions of the programme as they are already known (implement new core banking system, change payments provider, migrate back-book of accounts, launch innovative new BNPL offering, rationalise legacy product book, etc.).
    
-   Though certainly not a guarantee at this early stage of the programme lifecycle, where any decisions or assumptions have already been made for any of the other programme workstreams these should be inputs to the Product & Transition Roadmap process, as they may have a bearing on product go-live planning.
    

**Non-Product Inputs to the Product & Transition Roadmap**

Migration Inputs (the approach that will be taken for moving data from the legacy tech stack to the target tech stack)

-   Migration principles.
    
-   Overall Migration approach per product (run-off, on/offboard, phased, parallel run, etc.).
    
-   High-level tranching approach (unit of tranching, number of tranches, colleague pilots, etc.)
    
-   Whether there will be multiple migration transition states for different target systems and the sequencing of these (e.g. migrate data into Customer Core months ahead of Account Migration).
    
-   Migration delivery planning.
