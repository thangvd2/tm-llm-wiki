# Product Strategy

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The Product Strategy, once created, should be viewed as a foundational guide. It is a key conduit between the company business case and the needs of the customer. As such, it should be utilised as a reference point throughout the discovery and design process to ensure the constant adherence to the product vision.

Without this, it is extremely difficult to plan for a significant change to existing financial products or the introduction of new ones.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

The product strategy guides the development, positioning, and management of the products.

It involves making informed decisions about what products to develop, how to differentiate them, and how to deliver value to customers.

Below are the key elements of product strategy:

-   **Product Vision**: the product vision outlines the long-term purpose and product goals. It serves as a guiding star for the development team, aligning efforts with the broader mission and objectives. For example, is the intent to drive innovation, rationalise product portfolio, or meet a regulatory requirement better?
    
-   **Market Research and Analysis**: this involves understanding the market landscape, customer needs, competitor products, and potential market gaps.
    
-   **Customer Segmentation**: customer segmentation involves dividing the target market into distinct groups based on demographics, behaviours, needs, and preferences. This helps in tailoring products to meet specific customer needs.
    
-   **Value Proposition**: the value proposition defines the unique benefits and value that the products will offer to customers, differentiating it from competitors.
    
-   **Product Positioning**: product positioning involves creating a distinct image of the product in the minds of consumers, highlighting its unique benefits and how it meets their needs better than competitors.
    
-   **Product Prioritisation**: provides the detail on which potential products are being prioritised over the others, and the reasons for doing so. It should highlight what benefits the ordering brings, as this will drive the roadmap approach (ie. whether or not an MVP approach will be taken to test the waters first).
    
-   **Programme execution** - think big, start small! While it is important to think big about the programme’s strategic objectives and target state from the beginning, it is even more important to start small with manageable goals. That way quick wins and early returns on investment can be demonstrated. This is key to ensure that as the programme proceeds there is sufficient data to support the subsequent decisions to be made.
    

### [](#example "Copy link to heading")Example

Based on the guidance above, the strategy should look to answer the following questions:

1.  **What is the product vision?**
    
    -   Express the scope in alignment with the Governance workstream. (e.g. innovation, product rationalisation, regulatory requirement).
        
    
2.  **Who is the product for?**
    
    -   Provide the details on the customer segmentation, market research and customer insights.
        
    
3.  **Why has the product been chosen and what is the value proposition?**
    
    -   Detail how the product competes or differentiates itself in the market and what customer needs it will address, in alignment with the objectives from the Governance workstream.
        
    -   Detail how the introduction of a new product would avoid product cannibalisation of any existing products.
        
    
4.  **How will the products be prioritised and brought to market?**
    
    -   Is this greenfield, side-car, or modernisation of existing?
        
    -   Will an MVP be utilised?
        
    -   Is it Retail- or Business-banking focused, or both? How should product launches be ordered?
        
    
5.  **How will this be executed?**
    
    -   Identify the internal stakeholders required to collaborate, share insights and enable the execution, root and branch. It needs to consider new and affected products, processes and data and how they will be incorporated or adapted; all external party communications and any migration notion.
