# As is Process Discovery

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The bank is a business or multiple businesses made up of a plethora of processes for various outcomes, touching on many aspects such as tools, systems, documentation, teams and customers. These need to all work together seamlessly, correctly and productively to ensure the success of the bank.

A process can be anything, such as:

-   Open a current account
    
-   Provide a mortgage repayment schedule
    
-   Perform AML check
    
-   Generate a regulatory report
    

In the above, the first two examples are core ledger specific; whereas the last two, whilst not necessarily directly related to the core are still relevant for the modernisation programme. The third example is impacted by how the fraud system integrates with the core; the fourth is impacted by how data is streamed out of the core.

The purpose of this activity is to gain a detailed understanding of the existing processes in place that could potentially be affected by a modernisation exercise.

The benefit to performing this activity is that the outcome will be a well documented list of processes that can therefore be shared and understood by all relevant areas of the bank.

This in turn, can be helpful in identifying pitfalls within certain processes or highlight those that are no longer fit for purpose. A decision can then be made as to whether a particular process should be scheduled for either decommission or improvement. This decision can then be executed upon either as part of the programme or prior to the modernisation exercise.

The output of this activity could also highlight processes that could be rationalised or automated in the future, hence taking the initial steps in moving towards a process estate that is less complex, easier to maintain and more efficient.

From a modernisation exercise perspective, this activity is key to understanding what processes are absolutely necessary and hence will need to remain and how employees interact with these processes today. Understanding this will aid in driving the design for any new future processes with the added context of how employees would be impacted by them and expected to interact with them.

A longer term benefit of this outcome is feeding up-to-date data directly into a business process management system. This system should be overseen by a set of stringent governance procedures made up of a regular cadence of activities. The aim being to ensure process data is kept current, maintained, improved upon where necessary and where the details can subsequently be shared easily with the relevant team members and applications.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Process Discovery utilises a number of techniques to understand the current state of business processes. The output of which should give a clear view of all the details regarding the processes. This output can be split further into the following, for more clarity:

-   **Process Mapping** - this is a way to visualise a process to further understand how it works. Through swimlane or data flow diagrams it can be clearly seen which systems and users are involved, what their involvement is and how a hand-off is performed over to the next team/system. When overlayed with other business processes it can be easy to see any overlaps or repetitive behaviour.
    
-   **Process Documentation** - this is required to thoroughly detail the process and allow others to gain a full understanding. Without this, it is hard to know what the process is for and in what circumstances it should be executed and even how it should be executed.
    

How to undertake the Process Discovery activity:

1.  Pick a business vertical and identify the various user journeys
    
2.  Repeat this across the other business verticals
    
3.  Use various tools, documentation, relationships (ie. interviews with project managers, product owners, SMEs, component engineers, business analysts, etc.) to search/gain access to details of the processes. This could also include creating focus groups, observing teams as they run their processes or applying process mining techniques.
    
4.  Identify with which systems the process integrates
    
5.  Identify known performance metrics and any SLAs, SLOs and SLIs
    
6.  Identify which departments/teams the process impacts and who owns the process
    
7.  Map out each step of the process, visually ie. applying BPMN modelling or UML interaction diagrams. Utilising the previously analysed data, exposing how the output of one step turns into the input of the next one. A mapping software tool can greatly help with this action as they tend to have templates to assist, helpful interfaces and allow collaboration so that teams can work on mapping a process together, expediting the action.
    
8.  Document what the process does, how it is triggered, its frequency and what business value it provides. It should have the detailed guidelines of how a process is executed and what it is for. Due to its completeness, it can serve as an important piece of training material for new joiners or even other teams who are not all that familiar with a specific part of the bank/business process.
    

### [](#tips "Copy link to heading")Tips

Take a SMART approach to the discovery analysis to ensure focus:

-   **Specific** - stick to processes that directly impact or are impacted by the core or cores
    
-   **Measurable** - have a way of measuring the progress of this activity
    
-   **Attainable** - ensure process owners and stakeholders have an understanding in what is being attempted and for when so that they can ensure availability of key SMEs.
    
-   **Relevant** - ensure the output is relevant to the process and the modernisation programme
    
-   **Time-bound** - this is extremely important as otherwise this exercise can take a long time to achieve and the relevance of it will likely become diminished over time
    

Use the opportunity to highlight any non standardised aspects to processes so that going forward consistency can be ensured, eg. in documentation etc.

It is possible to use process discovery software that can integrate with the bank’s systems providing a number of advantages, eg. being able to perform analysis quickly and following up by drawing the process flows based off the data. Some software can capture user behaviour in real-time and hence provide a more granular view of each step of the process with which the user interacts.

Some tasks within processes don’t always occur in the same way, ie. one colleague may have a different process for producing a report than another one. Being both aware of this and highlighting it when it arises ensures that the output of the activity is as thorough as possible.

There is no single approach that fits everything as even the types of processes themselves differ vastly depending on the business use case. Whilst it’s helpful for the output of the analysis to be consistent it is more helpful to perform the activity in full and in a way that can be well interpreted by others. That could mean having a variety of flowcharts, swimlanes and value stream maps, depending on what is being represented.
