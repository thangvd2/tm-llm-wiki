# Product Requirements Gathering

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The purpose of the Product Requirements Gathering activity is to define and document the specific features, functionalities, and characteristics of a financial product that meet the needs and expectations of its users and stakeholders. Here are some key purposes of product requirements gathering:

1.  **Understanding User Needs:** Gathering product requirements involves understanding the needs, preferences, and pain points of the product’s target users. By collecting feedback from users and stakeholders, product teams can ensure that the product addresses real-world problems and provides value to its intended audience.
    
2.  **Defining Scope and Objectives:** Product requirements gathering helps in defining the scope and objectives of the product development effort. It establishes clear boundaries for what the product will and will not include, helping to prevent scope creep and ensure that resources are allocated effectively.
    
3.  **Guiding Development:** Product requirements serve as a blueprint for the development team, providing clear guidance on what needs to be built. They outline the specific features, functionalities, and user interactions that the product should support, helping developers understand what is expected of them.
    
4.  **Facilitating Communication:** Requirements gathering facilitates communication and alignment among stakeholders, including product managers, designers, developers, marketers, and customers. By documenting requirements in a clear and structured manner, everyone involved in the product development process can have a shared understanding of what needs to be achieved.
    
5.  **Supporting Decision Making:** Product requirements provide a basis for making informed decisions throughout the product development lifecycle. They help prioritise features and functionalities based on their importance to users and the business, identify trade-offs, and allocate resources effectively.
    
6.  **Managing Expectations:** By documenting product requirements, teams can manage stakeholders' expectations regarding what the product will deliver and when it will be offered. This helps build trust and confidence among stakeholders and reduces the likelihood of misunderstandings or conflicts later in the development process.
    

Overall, product requirements gathering is essential for ensuring that the product meets the needs of its users and stakeholders, is developed efficiently, and delivers value to the business. It’s a foundational step in the product development process that lays the groundwork for successful execution and delivery.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Executing requirements gathering effectively involves a structured approach that engages stakeholders, gathers relevant information, and documents requirements in a clear and actionable manner. Here’s a quick guide on how to execute requirements gathering:

1.  **Identify Stakeholders:** Identify key stakeholders involved in or affected by the product development process. This may include participants from:
    
    -   Business - Business Heads / Owners, Product Managers
        
    -   Operations
        
    -   Finance
        
    -   Technology - Architects, Engineering leads, Testing Leads
        
    
2.  **Define Objectives:** Clarify the objectives of the requirements-gathering process. Determine what specific information you need to collect, such as:
    
    -   Functional Requirements including:
        
        -   User Journeys
            
        -   Financial Product Features & Associated Behaviours
            
        -   Data & Reporting requirements covered as a part of \[Data Requirements Gathering \]
            
        -   Accounting Requirements
            
        -   Operational Requirements, covered as part of [Process Requirements Gathering](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/business/process_requirements)
            
        -   Migration Requirements
            
        
    -   Non-Functional requirements include:
        
        -   Performance (TPS, RTT etc.)
            
        -   Number of Customers
            
        -   Number of Accounts
            
        -   Ramp up expectations YoY
            
        
    
3.  **Choose Gathering Techniques**: Select appropriate techniques for gathering requirements based on the project scope, timeline, and stakeholder preferences. Common techniques include:
    
    -   **Workshops and Focus Groups:** Workshops and focus groups bring together stakeholders in a collaborative setting to brainstorm ideas, discuss requirements, and prioritise features. These sessions promote active participation, sharing of ideas and building stakeholder consensus. At this stage of the project, this is the preferred approach, where the project teams should sit with the client stakeholders and conduct the requirements gathering and scoping exercise.
        
    -   **Document Analysis:** Document analysis involves reviewing existing documentation such as business plans, user manuals, process documents, and technical specifications to extract relevant requirements. This technique is useful for understanding the project’s context, constraints, and existing requirements. This can be requested from the client before getting into scoping workshops.
        
    -   **Use Cases and Scenarios:** Use cases and scenarios describe specific interactions or sequences of actions that users perform to achieve their goals. These techniques help identify functional requirements by focusing on the tasks that users need to accomplish and the system’s responses to those tasks. Encourage clients to start this activity before engaging into scoping discussions and workshops as that will drive better outcomes.
        
    
4.  **Prepare Documentation**: Develop documentation templates and guidelines to capture requirements effectively. At this stage, the expected outcome is to arrive at the product scope that needs to be delivered as per the planned project phases. Use tools like Jira, Confluence, etc. to facilitate better collaboration.
    
    -   While preparing for the requirements gathering workshop and in the absence of well-documented requirements shared by the customer. The set of documents below can be used to drive the product-scoping discussions
        
        -   Gap Analysis Templates - tailored for each Product Library product can drive product scoping discussions
            
        -   Product Library - Financial Product Guides can be used to explain the features in detail.
            
        
    
5.  **Conduct Sessions**: Once the documentation is prepared, schedule and conduct requirements gathering sessions with stakeholders according to the chosen techniques. Facilitate open discussions, encourage active participation, and ask probing questions to elicit detailed requirements. Another important aspect is to time box, drive conversations effectively, and promptly achieve desired outcomes.
    
6.  **Document Requirements**: Record and document all the requirements as an outcome of the requirements gathering workshops. At this stage, one should have a good idea about the scope of the financial product expected by the customer. Ensure that requirements are clear, concise, and structured, with appropriate categorisation, prioritisation, and traceability.
    
    -   Use techniques like MoSCoW to prioritise the requirements, which can help in finalising the product scope for different phases of the project
        
    -   Apply SMART criteria to ensure that the requirements are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound
        
    
7.  **Validate Requirements & Scope Sign Off**: Review and validate the requirements and the scope with stakeholders to ensure accuracy, completeness, and alignment with project objectives. Address any discrepancies, conflicts, or ambiguities through further discussions and clarification. Getting formal sign-offs on the product requirements and scope at this stage is a good practice.
    
8.  **Iterate and Refine**: Iterate on the requirements gathering process as needed based on feedback and changes in project requirements. Continuously refine and update the documentation to reflect evolving stakeholder needs and project scope. There will also be a need to track all the additional requirements as per the change request process which the project or delivery manager should be able to guide on.
    

A good outcome of this step is to have a clear understanding of the product scope and a high-level break up in the form of **user journeys and user stories**, essentially a full **backlog readiness** for low-level requirement gathering and the Smart Contracts feature mapping for the Vault Core configuration layer delivery.
