# MVP Proposition Definition

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The purpose of this Minimal Viable Product (MVP) Proposition Definition is to help the financial institution define what products they clearly want to launch first. This is done with input from the [Product Strategy](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/business/product_strategy).

Utilising the \`think big, start small' mindset allows the financial institution to quickly show early proof points and return on investment (ROI), which in turn enables further product build and rollout.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

When defining the product strategy and minimal viable products, there are a number of factors to consider, which include but are not limited to:

-   New to bank vs. existing to bank customers.
    
-   New products only (greenfield) vs. legacy product migration only vs. a combination of the two.
    
-   Existing region / branch footprint vs. new out-of-market footprint.
    
-   Deposits vs. lending.
    
-   Retail vs. business banking.
    

The decisions made above in the product strategy directly influence what products should be included in the MVP(s). In order to define the MVP, financial institutions identify various potential options (e.g. retail CASA first followed by credit cards, business lending and cash management).

Each option includes the below to help key bank stakeholders decide the best path forward for their MVP launch (and subsequent releases):

-   List of products that define the option. These will be most aligned to overall programme goals.
    
-   Pros / cons for the given option across both business and technology implications.
    
-   Key features defined for each product at a high level.
    
-   Expected qualitative benefits to the bank employees or customer.
    

The MVP should be scoped in a way such that a \`friends and family' release can be done in approximately four months or, if skipping \`friends and family', a market launch in six months. It is important to start with a small, manageable scope and then add from there (e.g. digital only customers first followed by branch later; retail checking and savings first with simple money movement, followed by full money movement and credit cards).

The market launch could be limited to a particular sub-region within the existing financial institution’s footprint, which is a common approach taken by clients. We typically see subsequent products and features iteratively released on a roughly quarterly basis. This can be a combination of new products and legacy back-book products.
