# SME Access

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Subject Matter Experts (SMEs), internal and/or external to the client organisation, play a crucial role in programme delivery by providing specialist knowledge that is essential for the successful planning, execution, and delivery of the programme’s objectives.

The role of the SME is to consult, advise, and share their knowledge across the programme team to ensure deliverables meet the needs of the stakeholders, legislation, policies, standards, and best practices.

The absence of or late involvement of SMEs increases the risk of delivery failure.

## [](#predecessor_activities "Copy link to heading")Predecessor Activities

1.  Governance: [Resourcing Strategy](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance/resourcing_strategy)
    

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

-   It is important to source credible SMEs who are knowledgeable and qualified for the role. In many instances, SMEs will reside in the client organisation, however there maybe a need to source them externally.
    
-   SMEs may formally be part of the programme team or called upon as required; this is part of the Activity: [Resourcing Strategy](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance/resourcing_strategy).
    
-   Whilst it is not always necessary to have full time SMEs on the programme team, part time SMEs or SMEs that are not formally part of the programme team can get easily diverted away from programme and availability can not be guaranteed; this should be factored into the programme plan to allow some slack where these resources are involved.
    
-   Key areas for SME involvement in a core banking programme are:
