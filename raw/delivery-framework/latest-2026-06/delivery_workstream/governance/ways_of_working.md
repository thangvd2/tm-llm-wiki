# Ways of Working

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Ways of Working lays out the principles, processes, and practices that guide how work is performed.

It supports successful delivery through:

-   Standardisation on how work is executed.
    
-   Clarity onprocesses, roles, tools and communication practices; this is particularly useful for new team members.
    
-   A cohesive environment for working within and across teams.
    
-   Efficiency through streamline processes and smoother delivery.
    

The absence of well-defined ways of working can undermine programme efficiency, create confusion, and increase the risk of failure

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Ways of working sets the tone for how the programme runs and a proper structure will safeguard from disjointed ways of working across and within the workstreams.

Typically this involves:

-   **Values:** Defining the culture that the team values that guides behaviours.
    
-   **Methodology:** Specifying the delivery practises the team will follow, such as Scrum, Kanban, Scaled Agile or a hybrid approach and the key ceremonies such as sprint planning, backlog refinement and retrospectives.
    
-   **Working arrangements:** Agreeing how resources and teams will work together for example co-location, remote or hybrid working.
    
-   **Collaboration & communication channels:** Determining how the team will communicate, including meeting cadences (e.g daily stand-ups, sprint planning, retrospectives) and preferred communication channels (e.g., Slack, Teams, email, face-to-face).
    
-   **Tooling:** Stipulating the tools that will be used to support delivery processes including requirements capture, code repositories, time reporting, status reporting, etc.
    
-   **Involve** the team in defining ways of working to encourage collaboration and buy-in.
    
-   **Foster** a culture of fast feedback, providing examples of its value and showing action on the feedback received.
    
-   **Avoid over-standardising** can limit team autonomy and innovation. Allow some flexibility in ways of working for workstreams/squads.
    
-   **Set core meeting hours** when working with teams across time zones to ensure everyone can attend within their working hours.
    
-   **An adaptable and scalable delivery methodology** allows the programme to grow and become more complex.
    
-   **Assess the cultural shift and training needed** to implement the delivery methodologies; adapting to a new methodology can be particularly challenging for organisations with traditional practices or working with disparate teams who have experience of using a diverse set of methodologies. For example, an Agile Coach may be a useful addition to the team in the early stages.
    
-   **Fully understanding and adoption of the methodology** is important, ensuring roles like product ownership are properly defined, resources allocated and perform the requirements of the role. Too many organisations \`do agile' without understanding what this means and assigning the associated roles and responsibilities.
    
-   **Enable information sharing** with third-party partners and there is clarity on data-sharing policies.
    
-   **Integrate with the wider organisation**, coordinating with shared services and other functions that may have different priorities or constraints. For example, coordinating with other teams on e2e testing cycles, fixing issues with legacy batch based systems, and once a quarter release cycles.
