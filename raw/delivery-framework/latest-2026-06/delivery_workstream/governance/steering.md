# Steering Committee

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The purpose of establishing a Steering Committee is to provide strategic guidance, oversight, and decision-making to ensure the successful delivery across the lifecycle of the programmes. It serves as a high-level governance body responsible for:

-   Ensuring effective governance, oversight, and accountability for programme outcomes and benefits.
    
-   Providing strategic direction and alignment with the broader client organisation’s goals and priorities.
    
-   Informing senior stakeholders on progress, financial status, and risks.
    
-   Making key decisions and taking corrective actions or providing direction on key challenges.
    
-   Acting as a point of escalation for lower level governance forums.
    
-   Mediation of conflict that might affect the outcomes or timelines of the programme.
    
-   Facilitating communication and collaboration among stakeholders and ensuring their interests are represented in key programme decisions.
    

The Steering Committee’s role is crucial to a programme’s overall success by ensuring it remains focused on strategic objectives, addresses key challenges and aligns senior stakeholders.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

The steering committee should comprise of key stakeholders and decision-makers from various parts of the organisation to ensure comprehensive oversight and strategic alignment.

Typical members include:

-   **Chairperson:** This is typically undertaken by the programme sponsor who has a vested interest in the success of the programme.
    
-   **Programme Manager:** Oversee project execution and provides updates on progress, challenges and risks. The Programme Manager may act as the secretary and support the chair in meetings.
    
-   **CIO/CTO:** Provides technical oversight, ensuring the programme aligns with the organisation’s technology strategy and standards and key escalation point for unblocking any technology issues.
    
-   **CFO/Financial Controller:** Oversees the financial aspects of the programme by ensuring the budget approval, managing and reporting on forecasts and cash flow, and evaluating the financial impact of decisions.
    
-   **Business Line Lead(s):** Senior representative from key business line(s) that will be directly impacted by the programme.
    
-   **Legal/Compliance:** Ensure that the programme adheres to legal and regulatory requirements.
    
-   **Third-Party Vendors/Implementation Partners:** Key vendors and system integrators may participate to ensure alignment on delivery and contractual commitments.
    
-   **Subject Matter Experts (SMEs):** If required, SME (internal or external) may provide specialist knowledge and guidance to support delivery.
    

The key steps to establishing an effective change control board are:

1.  **Define the role of the steering committee:**
    
    -   Does it have authority over the entire programme or a specific phase of the programme?
        
    -   How will the steering committee support the programme as it scales? Will there be sub-committees? For example, a steering committee for each business line implementation.
        
    -   Does it have ultimate decision making power or does it need to refer to other committees for certain decisions? Decisions include budget approval or delivery dependencies on other programmes.
        
    
2.  **Select members:**
    
    -   Identify key members of the steering committee; this is usually undertaken by the sponsor.
        
    -   Ensure roles such as Chair and Secretary are assigned and roles are balanced with an effectively sized committee.
        
    
3.  **Define and agree terms of reference with members:**
    
    -   How the steering committee is structured and will operate, including communication channels, sharing materials, making decisions and what happens when there is no consensus.
        
    -   Who the members are and their responsibilities, including if all members have decision-making authority or if any members have veto powers?
        
    -   What is the frequency and location of meetings and the process for handling decisions or escalations outside of scheduled committee meetings?
        
    

### [](#common_pitfalls "Copy link to heading")Common Pitfalls

-   **Lack of clear purpose**: Absence of well-defined objectives and clear terms of reference for the Steering Committee; this can lead to ambiguity and lack of focus.
    
-   **Unclear levels of decision making**: Lack of a clearly defined decision making authority leads to indecision, drift and ultimately wasted time and cost. Defining authority levels helps clarify who is responsible for what decisions, and how decisions at various levels impact overall outcomes.
    
-   **Insufficient stakeholder representation**: Without the right mix of knowledge and stakeholders, decisions made may not reflect the broad client organisation’s priorities or goals. A well balanced group of steering committee members ensures informed decisions.
    
-   **Over-reliance on the steering committee**: Using the steering committee for day-to-day delivery decisions rather than providing strategic direction. This can lead to micromanagement and inefficiency, ultimately slowing down delivery.
    
-   **Lack of transparency**: Hiding issues or delaying sharing bad news from steering committee members to avoid attention or for fear of criticism only prolongs the inevitable, often making it harder to course-correct or mitigate negative impacts. Tell it as it is and don’t make drama about small things!
    
-   **Too much telling**: Spending time reviewing written status reports is not a good use of the steering committee. Use the time wisely and utilise memorable demonstrations to exhibit progress and focus discussion on managing risks and taking decisions; this creates greater understanding and greater engagement from stakeholders.
    
-   **Failure to adapt**: Core-banking programmes are likely to span multiple years, change is inevitable during this period. A key role of the steering committee is to ensure that the programme remains aligned with changing circumstances both within the wider client organisation and externally. Adaptability is crucial for a programme’s sustainability and long-term success.
