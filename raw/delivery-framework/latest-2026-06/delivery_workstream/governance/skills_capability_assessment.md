# Skills Capability Assessment

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

A Skills Capability Assessment systematically evaluates an organisation’s existing skills, experience, competencies and capacity against what is required to deliver the programme. The Purpose of a Programme Skills Capability Assessment is to:

-   **Assure delivery confidence:** Identify if the current team possesses the appropriate skill mix.
    
-   **Highlight risks:** Pinpoint where capability gaps might negatively affect timelines, quality, or outcomes.
    
-   **Inform resourcing decisions:** Guide choices related to hiring, training, or engaging external partners.
    
-   **Baseline maturity:** Establish a foundation for continuous capability development.
    

Failure to undertake this activity runs the risk of not having the necessary skills at a critical time to deliver the programme.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Once the [Programme Definition](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance/programme_definition) and [Delivery and Phasing Plan](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance/delivery_and_phasing_plan) have been developed, it is necessary to understand the skills required to deliver the programme, across all stakeholder groups inclusive of the business, change and technology functions, in order to ensure the programme team has the capability to deliver the programme’s strategic objectives.

This activity specifically focuses on identifying required skills, assessing their existing presence within the organisation, and critically, addressing any gaps. It also considers the volume of resources needed for specific skills, their availability and factors like geographical requirements.

It is important to consider some of the drivers for conducting a skills capability assessment. Performing an assessment in light of these outcomes will help to ensure the focus of the skills assessment is not too narrow e.g. limited to technology skills.

  
| Driver | Rationale | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 
**Identify Current-State Gaps**

 | 

-   Core banking delivery introduces new platforms, paradigms (e.g. event-driven, cloud-native), and delivery models (e.g. agile, DevOps).
    





 | 

-   Understand the skills gap between current capabilities and what’s required to deliver and operate the target-state platform.
    





 |
| 

**Inform Training and Upskilling Plans**

 | 

-   Resources will need to support new systems, processes, and tools.
    





 | 

-   Define training roadmaps, onboarding for new technologies (e.g. microservices, CI/CD, ISO 20022), and targeted learning paths by role.
    





 |
| 

**De-risk Programme Delivery**

 | 

-   Inadequate capability is a key delivery risk, especially in critical banking environments subject to regulatory oversight.
    





 | 

-   Surface where external support (e.g. contractors, system integrators) may be needed to address insufficiencies.
    





 |
| 

**Enable Effective Resource Planning**

 | 

-   Programmes often require redeployment of skilled staff or recruitment of new talent.
    





 | 

-   Define resourcing models for each phase (discovery, build, test, migration, run) and avoid bottlenecks.
    





 |
| 

**Ensure Organisational Readiness**

 | 

-   New systems often fail post-implementation due to a mismatch between operational needs and actual support capability.
    





 | 

-   Ensure operational teams are prepared to operate the new platform, minimising post-go-live risk.
    





 |
| 

**Support Regulatory and Governance Requirements**

 | 

-   Regulators (e.g. EBA, FED, FCA…) expect banks to manage operational resilience, skills adequacy, and change risk.
    





 | 

-   Provide evidence of due diligence in managing 'people risk' as part of operational resilience and change governance.
    





 |
| 

**Enhance Stakeholder Alignment**

 | 

-   Misalignment between business, IT, and change teams often causes delays and rework.
    





 | 

-   Foster shared understanding of roles, identify champions in each domain, and clarify where cross-functional capability is needed.
    





 |
| 

**Support Vendor and Tool Selection**

 | 

-   Skills gaps may influence whether a bank can manage solutions in-house or requires external parties.
    





 | 

-   Drive decisions about build vs. buy, SaaS vs. Client Hosted, and vendor partnerships.
    





 |

### [](#common_pitfalls "Copy link to heading")Common Pitfalls

Avoid these frequent mistakes when assessing skills:

-   **Subjectivity:** Relying solely on self-assessments can lead to an inaccurate view of capabilities. Be objective and evidence-based.
    
-   **Narrow focus:** Neglecting to assess both technical and soft skills (e.g. communication, leadership, adaptability) can provide an incomplete picture.
    
-   **One-size-fits-all approach:** Failing to tailor skill assessments to the specific programme phase (e.g. greenfield vs migration, mobilisation vs. execution) can result in irrelevant findings.
