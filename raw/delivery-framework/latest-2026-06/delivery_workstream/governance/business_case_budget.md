# Business Case and Budget

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

The purpose of the business case and budget is to clearly communicate to stakeholders the rationale behind embarking on a Core change programme, providing a strong foundation for securing sponsorship. It builds on the [Programme Definition](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance/programme_definition) (the 'what')and the [Delivery & Phasing Plan](/delivery-framework/latest/EN/delivery_workstream/governance/delivery_and_phasing_plan) (the 'how')to ensure that the programme’s objectives are widely understood, with delivery options, costs, risks, and justifications agreed upon and thoroughly documented.

The business case serves as a baseline to measure whether the programme is on track to deliver its intended benefits, and to prompt corrective action if necessary to avoid failure.

By tightly coupling the business case with the budget, the programme ensures that funding for the entire scope is clearly understood and aligned with financial cycles. This allows the programme to sequence benefits relative to costs, optimising returns within the constraints of typical annual budget cycles.

Without a well-defined business case and strong sponsorship, programmes often lack clear direction and are more vulnerable to delays, interruptions, or under achieving on benefits delivery.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

-   A compelling business case and budget must present a strong rationale for initiating the programme by assessing various options and explaining the decision behind the preferred approach. It’s essential to dedicate sufficient time to identify all impacted business areas, particularly in larger organisations, to ensure a shared understanding and endorsement among stakeholders. This alignment is a key responsibility of the programme sponsor.
    
-   Securing the appropriate level of sponsorship at this stage is vital, as it not only initiates the programme but also guarantees ongoing support, guidance, and advocacy throughout the programme’s lifecycle. This support helps navigate challenges and maintain focus on delivering intended outcomes.
    
-   Multiple levels of business case may be required, typically aligned with funding sources and organisational approval processes. For example:
    
    -   A strategic (high-level) business case is used to initiate the overall programme, providing context and rationale for key decisions, such as selecting Vault Core.
        
    -   A phase (detailed) business case secures approval for specific phases, such as MVP delivery or migrating a particular business line. This case contains detailed plans and justifications for the specific phase.
        
    
-   The business case should be a dynamic document that evolves throughout the programme’s lifecycle. It must be regularly reviewed and updated to reflect changes in the business environment, scope, risks, and opportunities. This adaptability ensures the business case remains relevant, continues to provide robust justification for investment, and allows sponsors to reconfirm their commitment to the programme.
    

error

Failure to agree on a comprehensive business case and budget, and to achieve alignment among stakeholders, can result in disappointed sponsors and difficulties in securing budget for the programme’s full lifecycle. It also increases the chances that the programme does not deliver the maximum benefits for the organisation.

### [](#vault_core_tangible_benefits "Copy link to heading")Vault Core Tangible Benefits

The new technology stack, when delivered and fully operational, must deliver deep tangible benefits to the organisation, providing benefits to all the stakeholders.

It needs to be considered as more that just a technology programme, for example it needs to deliver cost savings (finance), market changing product opportunities (business lines), ease of maintenance and change (COI function). These benefits will vary from organisation to organisation.
