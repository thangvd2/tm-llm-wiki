# Execute Programme Controls

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Execute Programme Controls is the day to day management and monitoring of the programme through real-time information. The key benefits of having effective controls are:

-   **Improved outcomes:** ensures the programme stays on track in both time and cost, meets its objectives, and delivers the expected benefits to the required quality.
    
-   **Clear Accountability:** establishes responsibilities and holds individuals accountable for programme activities increasing the likelihood of on-time delivery that is in line with expectations.
    
-   **Faster and more informed decision-making:** provides timely data and governance structure to support informed decisions.
    
-   **Reduced risks:** proactive risk management minimises disruptions and ensures projects stay on course, increasing their likelihood of success.
    
-   **Increased Efficiency:** optimises resource utilisation, ensuring resources are used efficiently and effectively.
    
-   **Quality Assurance:** ensures products and services meet the required quality standards and customer expectations.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

Executing programme controls involves creating and implementing a structured approach to manage a programme’s cost, schedule, and risks, ensuring it aligns with strategic objectives.

Operating a programme control cycle follows a 4-stage process:

1.  **Establish a baseline:** define initial plans covering scope, timeline, budget, and key performance indicators; this provides something to measure against.
    
2.  **Monitor progress:** regularly collect and measure progress against the defined baseline.
    
3.  **Analyse and compare:** evaluate actual progress against the baseline and determine the cause of any forecasted or actual deviations from the original plan.
    
4.  **Correct deviations:** Implement remedial steps to fix identified variances using:
    
    1.  Risk management to mitigate or prevent potential issues from occurring.
        
    2.  Change control to assess, review and approve proposed changes and implement approved changes in a controlled manner (see Change Control Board).
        
    

### [](#example_programme_control_metrics "Copy link to heading")Example Programme Control Metrics

-   **Financial:** encompassing budget tracking, cost analysis, revenue tracking, cash flow management, and variance analysis.
    
-   **Benefits management:** measuring project performance, along with financial metrics like profitability and ROI.
    
-   **Schedule:** tracking task completion, milestones achieved, dependencies, and overall project progress.
    
-   **Risk:** identifying potential issues, assessing their impact, and outlining mitigation strategies.
    
-   **Resource:** making sure the right people are available to do the work when required and that everyone is utilised appropriately.
    
-   **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):** measuring the project’s performance against predefined goals (for example sprint burndown, team velocity, automated test coverage, number of open/closed defects, number of accounts migrated).
    

## [](#key_considerations_for_effective_programme_controls "Copy link to heading")Key considerations for effective programme controls

-   **Balance is essential:** Avoid overwhelming the Programme Team with excessive reporting and meetings, which can impede delivery.
    
-   **Tailor reporting frequency:** Align reporting frequency with each metric. Some metrics suit sprint-based reporting, while others may require monthly (financials) or more dynamic updates (e.g., open test defects).
    
-   **Prioritise automation:** Implement tools like JIRA for automated data collection, processing, and storage to minimise manual effort.
    
-   **Focus on relevant metrics:** Define and track only key performance indicators (KPIs) that directly relate to objectives, stakeholder needs and phase of the programme.
    
-   **Continuous Improvement:** Regularly review and refine controls processes to ensure they remain effective and efficient.
