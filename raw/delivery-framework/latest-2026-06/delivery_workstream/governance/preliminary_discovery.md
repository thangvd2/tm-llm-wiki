# Preliminary Discovery

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Preliminary discovery is the first stage of the programme. The activity objective is to define the problem statement and confirm viability to initiate a programme to solve it.

The output of this activity includes:

-   Definition of the problem statement, confirmation that it needs to be solved and in what timeframe (e.g. to achieve a regulatory deadline or the end of contract for the use of legacy software).
    
-   Alignment with the broader organisation’s landscape including business, product, and technology strategies.
    
-   Evaluation of technology, which may involve a proof of concept, to support system selection.
    
-   Assessment of the delivery capabilities of the client, vendors, and partners to understand how the change programme will need to be resourced for success.
    
-   Assessment of the intended approach to deliver the change programme.
    
-   Identification of stakeholders and obtaining initial buy-in.
    
-   Anticipated impact on the organisation and customers.
    
-   Consideration of time constraints, key dependencies, conflicting, or competing programmes in the organisation that may impact delivery.
    
-   Ascertaining indicative costs to be approved as part of the business case.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

-   There must be a clear definition of the problem that is trying to be solved. Without this, it is difficult to scope the programme, assess possible solutions and obtain the right stakeholder buy-in.
    
-   Early stakeholder engagement is key to ensuring there is sponsorship from day 1.
    
-   When embarking on a change programme, it’s essential to consider the following to ensure success:
    
    -   **Cloud capability:** Consider the organisation’s current cloud capability and experience of using cloud applications. Does the organisation have the necessary team and skills? This will influence the decision on cloud hosting choice - SaaS or client hosted.
        
    -   **Alignment with timelines:** Ensure that the proposed change can be delivered within the required timelines, such as meeting regulatory compliance dates, to avoid any unnecessary delays.
        
    -   **Consideration of concurrent initiatives:** Assess the landscape of ongoing change programmes within the organisation. Understanding the broader change environment helps in coordinating efforts, optimising resource allocation, and ensuring that key resources are available when needed.
        
    -   **Capacity for change:** Evaluate the organisation’s capacity to manage multiple change initiatives simultaneously. A well-paced approach prevents change saturation, which can enhance employee engagement and help the organisation effectively realise the intended benefits.
        
    -   **Dependency management:** Identify any key dependencies on other initiatives, vendors, or teams. Proactively managing these dependencies ensures smooth coordination and minimises risks that could impact the programme’s progress.
        
    -   **Financial Feasibility:** Consider the affordability of the programme within current and future budget constraints. Ensuring financial feasibility from the outset helps maintain momentum and continuity, enabling the programme to achieve its full potential without financial hurdles.
