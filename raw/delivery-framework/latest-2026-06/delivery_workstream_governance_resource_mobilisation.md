# Resource Mobilisation

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Resource mobilisation is an ongoing process driven by your Delivery and Phasing Plan and Resourcing Strategy. It involves selecting and onboarding the appropriate resources— internal and vendor personnel (including SME Access) and tooling—at the optimal time. This activity aims to:

-   Secure resources with the right skillsets and experience, aligned with the phase scope and objectives.
    
-   Ensure the right people and tools are available when needed, preventing delays or bottlenecks in delivery.
    
-   Maximise the efficient use of resources across the entire programme team.
    

Effective resource mobilisation requires meticulous planning, good communication, and a proactive approach to acquiring and managing resources.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

-   **Expedite Onboarding for Critical Roles:** Prioritise the swift onboarding of key personnel such as Programme Managers, Product owners, Architects and PMO. Their early participation is crucial for establishing the programme’s structure, plans, and governance before the full team is mobilized.
    
-   **Conduct a Programme Kick-off:** Schedule a formal team kick-off to officially commence phase delivery. This session ensures alignment among the programme team and wider stakeholders regarding the programme phase’s goals, structure, approach, and expectations. It sets the tone, direction, and momentum for the work ahead and provides an opportunity for team building. Key topics to cover include:
    
    -   Vision and objectives.
        
    -   Scope, timeline, and delivery approach.
        
    -   Programme team structure and governance.
        
    -   Ways of working.
        
    
-   **Prioritise In-Person Kick-off (If Feasible):** Whenever possible, conduct the kick-off in person. This approach significantly accelerates relationship building and improves communication within the team.
    
-   **Implement Ongoing Inductions:** For resources joining after the initial kick-off, ensure a comprehensive induction process to provide necessary context. Consider assigning a buddy to help them familiarise themselves with tools and ways of working, facilitating early productivity.
    
-   **Align with Thought Machine Partners:** Thought Machine briefs all partners as part of the mobilisation process to ensure alignment from the outset. This includes:
    
    -   Scope, priorities, and expectations.
        
    -   Commercial terms (e.g. scope, commercial model).
        
    -   Identified risks, dependencies, assumptions, and decisions to date.
        
    -   Clearly defined roles and responsibilities between Thought Machine and the Partner for service provision, preventing friction or duplication once delivery commences.
        
    -   Regular project governance forums
        
    
-   **Account for Onboarding Requirements and Lead Times:** Understand the organisational requirements and lead times for onboarding, including systems access. Allocate sufficient time to accommodate these factors, allowing for:
    
    -   Transitioning from previous roles.
        
    -   Systems onboarding.
        
    -   Completion of any necessary upskilling or mandatory training.
        
    

Note: For 3rd party resources, screening checks may be required for onboarding.

-   **Streamline Resource Request and Onboarding:** Define a clear and smooth process for requesting and onboarding resources with designated responsibilities that ensure that there is budget, procurement approvals and headcount.
    
-   **Remember SMEs:** Secure capacity from shared SME resources (e.g. Risk & Compliance, Cloud, Finance…) who are often not dedicated to the programme and are in high demand. Delays occur if these resources, are not mobilised promptly, creating dependencies and delays.
    

### [](#common_pitfalls "Copy link to heading")Common Pitfalls

Avoid these frequent mistakes when mobilising resources:

-   **Premature Onboarding:** Bringing on resources too quickly without established governance and ways of working from day one. This can lead to individuals creating their own processes, which are then difficult to alter and can cause tension.
    
-   **Underestimated Lead Times:** Misjudging the time required for procurement or resource onboarding, leading to project delays.
    
-   **Delayed Systems Access:** Insufficient or slow access to necessary systems, which reduces productivity.
    
-   **Over-reliance on Temporary Solutions:** Depending too heavily on workarounds, such as manual tools instead of integrated platforms, or a small group of individuals handling a disproportionate amount of work without adequate capacity or depth of knowledge.
    
-   **Competing Priorities:** When resources are shared across teams (both internal and external to the programme), competing priorities for their time can arise. It is crucial to establish clear terms from the outset for how work is assigned and priorities are managed.
