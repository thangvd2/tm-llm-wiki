# As is Product Discovery

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

This is a key activity especially if the bank wants/needs to rationalise a large portfolio of products into fewer, manageable products that allow for bespoke functionality and simpler maintenance.

This activity can also prove to be highly beneficial if the bank just wants to have a clear view of their current portfolio particularly since over time orphaned, redundant products will remain and multiple variants of the same product exist for varying reasons eg. due to lack of functionality in an existing core, deficiencies with configuration or lack of ease with enriching the products.

The output of this activity can prove invaluable for many use cases, such as:

-   Understanding the existing customers' usage and take up volumes.
    
-   Understanding the customer segmentation.
    
-   Plan for canvassing existing or new potential customers with a view to understanding the key user journeys.This can in turn also help to identify any feature gaps in the product which can then be prioritised for build based on this data driven approach.
    
-   Removing redundant products, leading to a cleaner product portfolio.
    

Without performing this activity it can be impossible to understand the full extent to which varieties of products currently exist, which are currently being actively used and which have become totally redundant. The maintenance of the products will become burdensome which is likely to become a barrier to any potential future product innovation.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

The most logical way to achieve this is by performing the following:

1.  *Identify the various places where the financial products may be held* - if there are multiple cores and multiple business verticals, getting access to the data could require specific authorisation.
    
2.  *If there are silo’d products, understand the reasons as to why this is so* - understanding this is key to planning a potential product rationalisation.
    
3.  *Categorise the various products by product type* - this will highlight how many variants of the same type of product exist and therefore which could potentially be removed in the future.
    
4.  *If possible, identify which of the products are currently active* - this will not only help to narrow down the amount of analysis required but also provide a clearer view as to the existing customer segmentation and customer behaviour.
    
5.  *Note down the individual features associated with the products* - this will help understand what the products can do today and help identify any missing features that can be added as part of a future innovation exercise.
    
6.  *Gather customer feedback on the products* - understand the user experience and expectations. Identify what it is about the particular product that makes it desirable to customers, understanding this is important to ensure that anything that gets rationalised in the future still takes these features into consideration.
    
7.  *Overlay these features against the product’s specific terms and conditions* - there could be an opportunity here to condense the product data if some features are not being used and, conversely, it will also help the understanding, in the case of product rationalisation, of which features absolutely need to remain. This could even provide an opportunity to review and simplify/innovate on the product’s terms and conditions.
    
8.  *Clarify the value proposition* - highlight what makes the product key and which area of the market it is addressing. Understanding if it is a differentiator ensures that this is taken into consideration if an objective of the programme is to rationalise the product estate.
