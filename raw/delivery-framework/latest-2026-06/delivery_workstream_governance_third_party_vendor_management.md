# Third Party Vendor Management

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

Third Party Vendor Management defines and implements a robust framework for managing third-party vendors who contribute critical products, services, or expertise to the programme. This includes the processes and governance required to ensure vendors are selected appropriately, onboarded effectively, managed consistently, and off-boarded securely, in order to support the successful delivery of programme objectives.

Third Party Vendor Management ensures that vendor-related risks are actively monitored, contractual obligations are met, and performance is measured and optimised throughout the programme lifecycle.

## [](#objective "Copy link to heading")Objective

To establish and execute a structured approach for identifying, assessing, onboarding, managing, and off-boarding third-party vendors who play a critical role in the delivery of the programme. This ensures that vendor relationships are governed effectively, risks are mitigated, and vendors contribute successfully to the programme’s objectives.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

This activity should be led collaboratively by the programme team in coordination with Procurement, Legal, Risk, and relevant business stakeholders. Third-party management must be integrated into the overall programme governance structure, with appropriate representation in forums such as the Steering Committee, Change Control Board, and Design Authority where applicable.

Vendor engagement should be underpinned by clear communication, mutual accountability, and continuous performance oversight. This means going beyond transactional procurement and embedding vendors into delivery workflows, stakeholder management routines, and risk mitigation strategies.

Particular attention should be paid to:

-   The strategic importance of the vendor’s deliverables;
    
-   Data privacy and regulatory compliance obligations;
    
-   Intellectual property ownership of deliverables;
    
-   Capability and scalability of vendor(s) to meet delivery needs; and
    
-   Exit planning in case of vendor failure or contract completion.

## [](#key_activities_and_deliverables "Copy link to heading")Key Activities and Deliverables

-   **Vendor Strategy Definition**
    
    -   Define which products, services, or capabilities will be sourced externally.
        
    -   Develop the vendor engagement model and determine selection criteria.
        
    -   Align with procurement policy and programme sourcing strategy.
        
    
-   **Vendor Identification and Assessment**
    
    -   Conduct a Request for Information (RFI) or Request for Proposal (RFP) process as needed.
        
    -   Evaluate vendors based on commercial, operational, technical, and compliance criteria.
        
    -   Perform due diligence including financial health, security posture, and references.
        
    
-   **Contracting and Onboarding**
    
    -   Develop and finalise contracts, incorporating SLAs, KPIs, risk clauses, and exit terms.
        
    -   Onboard vendors into the programme organisation with access to tooling, documentation, and reporting standards.
        
    -   Provide induction sessions to ensure shared understanding of the programme vision and operating model.
        
    
-   **Performance Management**
    
    -   Monitor delivery progress and adherence to service levels through structured reporting and governance.
        
    -   Conduct periodic performance reviews with vendors (e.g. monthly, quarterly).
        
    -   Raise, track, and resolve any issues or non-conformities promptly.
        
    
-   **Risk & Compliance Oversight**
    
    -   Maintain a third-party risk register and ensure vendors meet regulatory and security obligations.
        
    -   Embed vendor risk in programme-wide risk management and escalation processes.
        
    -   Audit compliance as necessary and respond to findings proactively.
        
    
-   **Change Control**
    
    -   Ensure any changes to vendor scope, budget, or delivery timelines are governed through the programme’s Change Control Board.
        
    -   Assess vendor impact during programme-wide change assessments.
        
    
-   **Off-boarding & Transition Management**
    
    -   Execute structured off-boarding processes with handover of knowledge, data, and access removal.
        
    -   Capture lessons learned and formally close vendor contracts.
        
    -   Ensure continuity of service or delivery as vendors exit.
        
    

## [](#governance_and_integration "Copy link to heading")Governance and Integration

Vendor management must be represented within the overall programme governance framework. Strategic vendors may attend select governance forums or be assigned joint accountability roles for delivery. Key governance layers include:

-   **Vendor Relationship Manager:** A named individual responsible for day-to-day relationship and delivery coordination.
    
-   **Vendor Performance Reviews:** Regular (e.g. monthly) checkpoints to assess SLAs, KPIs, and risks.
    
-   **Joint Governance Forums:** Inclusion of key vendors in steering or working group sessions.
    
-   **Issue & Risk Escalation:** Clearly defined and documented routes for raising and resolving issues.
    

## [](#risks_and_mitigation "Copy link to heading")Risks and Mitigation

 
| Risk | Mitigation Strategy |

 | 

Include Vault implementation experience and number of Vault certified resources as part of the assessment criteria.

 |

Misaligned expectations or scope

 |

Conduct onboarding and joint planning workshops.

 |
| 

Lack of delivery performance

 | 

Encourage two-way communication with regular governance forums and reviews.

Define acceptance criteria, SLA and escalation paths.

 |
| 

Compliance breach or data exposure

 | 

Ensure vendors area aware of compliance policies and resource complete compliance training when mobilising the team.

Integrate with InfoSec, DPO, and Legal during selection and monitoring

 |
| 

Single vendor dependency

 | 

Develop contingency plans or dual-sourcing for critical services.

Ensure regular knowledge transfer/ training for the internal team to reduce operational reliance.

 |

## [](#best_practices "Copy link to heading")Best Practices

-   Define tiered governance for critical vs. non-critical vendors.
    
-   Conduct onboarding as a formal process, not an informal handoff.
    
-   Use shared tooling for reporting and collaboration (e.g. Jira, Confluence).
    
-   Treat strategic vendors as partners through joint planning and clear ownership of milestones.
    
-   Include vendors in programme health checks and retrospectives where appropriate.
    

## [](#success_criteria "Copy link to heading")Success Criteria

-   Vendors deliver against agreed contractual commitments and SLAs.
    
-   Vendors are fully integrated into programme planning, reporting, and governance.
    
-   All vendor risks are actively managed and tracked with strong working relationships enabling timely delivery and issue resolution.
    
-   Contracts are closed without dispute or service interruption.
