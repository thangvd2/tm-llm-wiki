# Health Checks

## [](#purpose "Copy link to heading")Purpose

A health check is a structured evaluation of the current state of a programme or individual components of it, offering valuable insights into its progress, successes, and areas requiring attention or enhancement.

Objectives of a health check:

-   **Independent evaluation:** To deliver an impartial evaluation of the programme’s or component’s performance, benchmarking against goals and ensuring alignment with organisational processes and standards.
    
-   **Identify strengths and weaknesses:** To highlight areas of success, as well as potential issues that may need to be addressed.
    
-   **Support proactive decision-making:** To inform the development of risk mitigation strategies and facilitate preemptive decision-making, thereby minimising potential issues.
    
-   **Ensure efficiency:** To confirm that scheduled deliveries remain on track to meet deadlines and stay within budgetary constraints, mitigating the risk of delays or cost overruns.
    

Expected outcomes of a health check:

-   **Comprehensive Report:** The generation of a detailed analytical report encompassing status, achievements, areas for improvement, potential risks, and recommendations.
    
-   **Action Plans:** The formulation of explicit action plans to address identified issues, manage risks, and pursue opportunities for optimisation.
    
-   **Updated Delivery Plans:** The revision of schedules, budgets, and plans to reflect adjustments recommended as a result of the Health Check findings.
    

**Consequences of Neglecting Health Checks:** Failure to conduct regular Health Checks may lead to missed opportunities for enhancement and increased project risks. The absence of routine assessments elevates the risk of programme failure, potentially resulting in increased costs, delays due to late interventions, reduced success and potential reputational damage.

## [](#guidance "Copy link to heading")Guidance

A health check focused on a specific component of the programme provides re-assurance, particularly if the component is delivered by a third party and/or the expertise does not reside within the organisation, for example Smart Contract or Vault infrastructure, responsibility is being handed over to them or to another vendor. This facilitates a smooth transition of accountability.

Different approaches for execution:

-   **Independent Assessors:** Health Checks are most effective when conducted by independent assessors, free from internal biases, to ensure a balanced and objective perspective. This approach enhances the likelihood that recommendations will be accepted and implemented.
    
-   **Structured Assessments:** Health checks should follow a structured approach, using established frameworks such as PMBOK. The organisation’s unique nature, project management and methodologies should also be taken into account to ensure the assessment is relevant.
    

Key general points:

-   **Independence:** Assessments should be conducted by parties external to the programme to maintain objectivity; this could be by another programme manager, an internal auditor within the organisation or by an external consultant.
    
-   **Approach:** Define clear assessment criteria and methodologies tailored to the programme or component under reviews objectives, context and challenges. Health checks should follow a structured approach, using established frameworks such as PMBOK or ITIL, where available taking into account the client’s methodologies, processes and procedures to ensure the assessment is relevant.
    
-   **Frequency:** Health checks should be conducted at pre-determined intervals based on specific delivery events or as challenges arise to help identify issues and opportunities for improvement in a timely manner.
    
-   **Data gathering:** Relevant data and information should be collected from project documentation, team members and stakeholders to ensure a comprehensive evaluation.
    
-   **Analysis:** Analyse the findings to identify areas of strength, weaknesses and potential risks that could impact the programme’s or component’s success.
    
-   **Communication:** The results of the assessment, along with any recommendations, should be communicated clearly to stakeholders, and action plans developed to address identified issues and risks.
    

Common pitfalls:

-   **Incomplete or biased data:** Relying on incomplete or biased data can undermine the credibility of the health check findings.
    
-   **Over-reliance on subjective assessments:** Relying too heavily on subjective assessments without objective evidence may lead to inaccurate conclusions and recommendations.
    
-   **Failure to address early warning signs:** Ignoring early warning signs of potential problems may lead to project failure or missed opportunities for improvement.
    
-   **Lack of stakeholder involvement:** Failing to involve key stakeholders can result in resistance to or misunderstanding of the recommended actions.
    
-   **Neglecting follow-up:** Not following up on action plans or recommendations can undermine the effectiveness of the health check process and diminish its long-term value.
